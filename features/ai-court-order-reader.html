Problem vs solution

What changes with Legal Staff

The problem

Reading every order by hand

  • Orders arrive in every shapePDFs downloaded from court websites, scans of certified copies, text forwarded by a clerk. Each one has to be opened and read end to end.
  • The next date hides in the last paragraphA fixed date, “list after four weeks”, “to be listed along with the connected matter” — easy to misread at the end of a long court day.
  • Dates are typed twiceEvery date is copied again into a paper diary or spreadsheet. One transposed digit and a hearing is missed.

With Legal Staff

Orders read for you, dates confirmed by you

  • Summary with paragraph referencesWhat was directed, what compliance is due and by whom, each point tied to the paragraph it came from so you can check it in seconds.
  • Next date picked out, held for approvalLegal Staff proposes the next hearing date and purpose. You confirm or correct it, and only then does it go into the diary and reminders.
  • Filed against the right caseThe order is saved in the case’s document vault, so the junior or clerk who opens the matter next sees the same order and the same summary.

How it works

From first step to daily habit

  1. Open or create the case

    Add the parties, court and case number. Where a court-data provider is connected, you can import case details using the CNR.

  2. Paste or upload the order

    Paste the text of the order or upload the PDF or scan you downloaded or received.

  3. Read the summary

    Directions, compliance and the next date are listed with the paragraph each one comes from.

  4. Confirm the next date

    Accept the proposed date and purpose, or correct them. The confirmed date goes into the court diary.

  5. Let reminders and the team follow

    Reminders are scheduled from the confirmed date, and the matter shows up on the hearing board for whoever is assigned.

Frequently asked questions

Does Legal Staff fetch orders automatically from court websites?

No. eCourts does not offer an open public API, so Legal Staff does not scrape court websites. You paste or upload the order. Where a court-data provider is connected, case details can be imported by CNR. The official court website remains the source of record.

Will a date go into my diary without my approval?

No. Legal Staff proposes the next date and purpose from the order, but nothing reaches the calendar or triggers reminders until an advocate confirms it.

What happens when the order has no fixed date?

Orders such as “list after four weeks” or “to be listed with the connected matter” do not name a date. The summary shows the direction so you can set the date once the listing is published, or add a follow-up task.

Which formats can I use?

You can paste the text of an order or upload a PDF or a scanned copy. Clear scans read better than faint photocopies, and you should always check the summary against the order itself.

Can it handle long judgments?

The summary works with paragraph references, so you can jump to the part that matters. For detailed questions on a long judgment in a case, use the AI Case Brain, which answers from the case’s own documents with citations.

Is the summary legal advice?

No. It is a reading aid. An advocate must review the summary and the proposed date against the order before acting on them.

Who can see the orders I upload?

Only members of your firm, according to the roles you set. Each firm’s data is isolated with row-level security and documents are kept in private storage.

Do I still need to check the cause list?

Yes. Listings can change after an order is passed. Keep checking your court’s cause list for the day; Legal Staff keeps the confirmed date, purpose and reminders organised around it.
