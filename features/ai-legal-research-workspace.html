How it works

From first step to daily habit

  1. Keep the file in the case

    Orders, pleadings and annexures uploaded to the case vault are what the Case Brain reads.

  2. Ask a question

    Ask in plain English: “When was the interim order last extended?” or “What did the respondent say about delay?”

  3. Open the citations

    Each answer cites the document and paragraph it relies on, so you can read the original passage.

  4. Look up a provision

    Search the reference notes for a quick note on a section, then check the bare Act.

  5. Draft the hearing brief

    Generate a draft brief for the next date, then edit and approve it before the hearing.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a case-law database?

No. The AI Case Brain answers from your case’s own documents, and the research section holds reference notes on provisions. Use a case-law database or official reports for precedents.

Can the AI make up judgments or citations?

It is built to answer only from the documents in the case and to cite them; it does not invent case law. Always open the citation and check the original before relying on an answer.

What if the answer is not in the file?

The Case Brain only uses the case’s documents, so if they do not cover a point, research it in the bare Act, reports or a case-law database.

Can I file or cite AI output directly?

No. Treat it as a draft or a pointer. Cite the original order, pleading or authority after you have read it, and have an advocate review anything that goes to court.

Does it cover BNS, BNSS and BSA?

The reference notes cover statutory provisions, and the free IPC to BNS converter maps old sections to new ones. The new codes have been in force since 1 July 2024; always check the bare Act for the exact text.

Is my case file shared with other firms?

No. Each firm’s data is isolated with row-level security, and documents are kept in private storage visible only to your team according to their roles.

What does a hearing-brief draft contain?

A summary of the matter and recent orders drawn from the case documents, with references, for you to edit. It is a starting point, not a finished brief.
