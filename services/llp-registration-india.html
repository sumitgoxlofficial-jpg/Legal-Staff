Overview

How LLP registration works

A Limited Liability Partnership is formed under the LLP Act, 2008. It is a separate legal entity whose partners' liability is limited to their agreed contribution, while the internal relationship is governed by an LLP agreement rather than by articles and board meetings. That makes it a popular choice for professional firms, consultancies, family businesses and joint ventures that do not need equity investors.

The process starts with RUN-LLP, which reserves the name, followed by FiLLiP (Form for incorporation of LLP). An LLP needs at least two designated partners who are individuals, and at least one of them must be resident in India. Designated partners without a DIN are allotted a DPIN through FiLLiP; they also need DSCs to sign.

After incorporation, the LLP agreement must be filed in Form 3 within 30 days. The agreement is stamped under the stamp law of the state where the LLP is registered, usually based on the capital contribution. We draft the agreement around your real arrangement — contribution, profit share, partner remuneration and interest, admission and exit, decision-making and dispute resolution — instead of a generic template.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum number of partners for an LLP?

At least two partners, and at least two designated partners who are individuals. At least one designated partner must be resident in India. There is no maximum number of partners.

What if the LLP agreement is not filed within 30 days?

Form 3 is due within 30 days of incorporation. After that, an additional fee of ₹100 per day of delay applies, and it keeps accruing until you file. Until an agreement is filed, the default provisions in the First Schedule to the LLP Act govern the partners' relationship, which may not reflect what you agreed.

Is there a minimum capital contribution for an LLP?

No minimum contribution is prescribed. Partners can start with a modest contribution and increase it later by amending the agreement and filing Form 3 again. The contribution affects FiLLiP fees and stamp duty.

How is an LLP taxed?

An LLP is taxed as a firm at a flat 30%, plus surcharge where income exceeds ₹1 crore and health and education cess. The share of profit received by partners is exempt in their hands, and remuneration and interest paid to partners are deductible within the limits of the Income-tax Act if the agreement authorises them — another reason the agreement must be drafted carefully.

When does an LLP need an audit?

Accounts must be audited if turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh in a financial year. A tax audit may separately apply under the Income-tax Act once turnover crosses the applicable limit.

What are the annual filings for an LLP?

Form 11 (annual return) by 30 May and Form 8 (statement of account and solvency) by 30 October each year, plus the income-tax return. Late filing attracts an additional fee of ₹100 per day per form, so a dormant LLP that ignores filings builds up large dues.

Can an LLP raise venture capital?

An LLP cannot issue shares, so it does not suit equity investment or ESOPs. If you expect to raise venture funding, start as a Private Limited company, or plan to convert the LLP into a company later under section 366 of the Companies Act.
