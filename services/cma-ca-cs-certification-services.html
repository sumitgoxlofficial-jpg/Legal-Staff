Overview

Which certificates we handle, and who may sign them

Many MCA forms must be certified by a practising professional. SPICe+ Part B carries both a declaration by the professional engaged in the formation and a certificate from a Chartered Accountant, Company Secretary or Cost and Management Accountant in practice. Other event-based forms, such as INC-20A, share allotment and charge filings, carry similar certifications. The professional who signs is confirming that they verified the facts against the records, so we check the underlying documents before we certify.

Cost and Management Accountants have specific roles. CAS-4, the cost accounting standard on cost of production for captive consumption, is used to value goods transferred between units or related parties; under GST valuation rules such supplies are commonly valued at 110% of cost of production, so a CAS-4 certificate is the evidence. Companies in specified sectors must maintain cost records in Form CRA-1 once their turnover crosses the threshold in the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules, 2014, and larger ones need a cost audit: appointment intimated in CRA-2, the report in CRA-3 and filing with MCA in CRA-4.

We also prepare CMA (Credit Monitoring Arrangement) data and project reports for working-capital and term loans, and valuation reports. Note the legal line here: valuations required under the Companies Act — for example for preferential allotment of shares — must be done by a Registered Valuer registered with IBBI, while FEMA pricing for foreign investment can be certified by a Chartered Accountant, a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practising Cost Accountant. We tell you which one your transaction needs before you pay for the wrong report.

Frequently asked questions

Who can certify SPICe+ Part B?

A Chartered Accountant, Company Secretary or Cost and Management Accountant in whole-time practice can certify the form. The declaration by the professional engaged in the formation can also be given by an advocate. The certifying professional must have verified the documents and is accountable to their institute for the certificate.

What is a CAS-4 certificate and when is it required?

CAS-4 is the cost accounting standard for determining the cost of production of goods used for captive consumption. It is commonly needed when goods move between distinct persons under GST (for example, units in different states) or to related parties where there is no transaction value, so the supply is valued on cost. The certificate shows how the cost was built up from materials, labour and overheads.

When must a company maintain cost records or get a cost audit?

Cost records apply to companies producing goods or providing services listed in the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules, 2014 when overall turnover in the preceding year is ₹35 crore or more. Cost audit applies at higher thresholds that differ for regulated and non-regulated sectors and also depend on the turnover of the individual product or service. We check applicability product by product.

What are CRA-2 and CRA-4?

CRA-2 is the intimation to the Central Government of the cost auditor's appointment, filed within 30 days of the board meeting that appointed them or within 180 days of the start of the financial year, whichever is earlier. CRA-4 is the filing of the cost audit report within 30 days of the company receiving it from the cost auditor.

Do I need a Registered Valuer or can a CA or CMA value my shares?

It depends on the purpose. Valuations required by the Companies Act, such as for preferential allotment or sweat equity, must be done by an IBBI Registered Valuer. For FEMA pricing on issue or transfer of shares involving non-residents, a Chartered Accountant, SEBI-registered merchant banker or practising Cost Accountant can value using an internationally accepted method. Tax rules have their own requirements, so we confirm before starting.

What is CMA data for a bank loan?

Credit Monitoring Arrangement data is the set of historical and projected financial statements, ratios, fund flow and working-capital assessment banks use to sanction or renew limits. It is not a statutory certificate, but banks expect it to be prepared or reviewed by a professional and to reconcile with your audited accounts.

What is UDIN and why does it matter?

UDIN (Unique Document Identification Number) is generated by the professional on their institute's portal for each certificate or report they sign. It lets banks, regulators and counterparties verify that the document was genuinely issued by that professional. Many authorities reject certificates that do not carry one.
