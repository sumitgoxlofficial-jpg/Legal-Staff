Overview

What annual ROC compliance covers

Every company registered under the Companies Act, 2013 must file its financial statements and annual return with the Registrar of Companies, whether or not it did any business. Financial statements go in AOC-4 within 30 days of the AGM, and the annual return in MGT-7 (or the abridged MGT-7A for small companies and OPCs) within 60 days of the AGM. The AGM itself must be held within six months of the end of the financial year — by 30 September for most companies — and the first AGM within nine months of the close of the first financial year.

Around these sit the event and periodic filings that are easy to miss: director KYC in DIR-3 KYC, the auditor's appointment in ADT-1 within 15 days of the AGM where an auditor is appointed, the annual return of deposits and exempted loans in DPT-3 by 30 June, and the half-yearly MSME-1 where the company owes micro or small enterprises for more than 45 days. Board meetings must be held at the required frequency and minuted, and statutory registers kept up to date.

Late annual filings attract an additional fee of ₹100 per day per form with no upper limit, on top of penalties under the Act. Persistent non-filing can lead to directors being disqualified and the company being struck off. Our plan maps every date for your company, prepares the documents, coordinates with your statutory auditor and files on time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the due date for AOC-4 and MGT-7?

AOC-4 is due within 30 days of the AGM and MGT-7 or MGT-7A within 60 days of the AGM. For a company holding its AGM on 30 September, that means AOC-4 by 30 October and MGT-7 by 29 November. OPCs, which do not hold an AGM, file AOC-4 within 180 days of the end of the financial year.

What is the penalty for late filing of annual returns?

An additional fee of ₹100 per day of delay applies to each of AOC-4 and MGT-7, with no maximum. Separate penalties can apply to the company and officers under the Act, and continued default for three years can disqualify directors under section 164(2).

Who must file DIR-3 KYC?

Every individual holding a DIN, whether or not they are currently a director of any company, must complete director KYC. If it is not filed by the due date the DIN is marked deactivated and reactivation requires the late fee, currently ₹5,000. MCA has revised the KYC frequency and process from time to time, so check the current rule each year — we track it for our clients.

Do small companies need a statutory audit?

Yes. Every company, including small companies and OPCs, must have its financial statements audited by a Chartered Accountant regardless of turnover. Small companies benefit from other relaxations, such as filing the abridged MGT-7A and fewer mandatory board meetings.

How many board meetings are required in a year?

A company must hold at least four board meetings a year with no more than 120 days between two meetings. OPCs, small companies and dormant companies can instead hold at least one meeting in each half of the calendar year with a gap of at least 90 days between them; private companies recognised as startups have similar relaxations.

What is DPT-3 and does my company need to file it?

DPT-3 is the return of deposits and of outstanding receipts of money or loans that are not deposits, such as loans from directors. Most companies that have such amounts outstanding at 31 March must file it by 30 June. Loans from directors must be backed by a declaration that the money is not borrowed by the director.

Is ROC compliance required if the company had no business this year?

Yes. A company with no transactions still needs audited accounts, an AGM, AOC-4, MGT-7/MGT-7A and director KYC. If the company will not be used, consider applying for dormant status or closing it through strike-off rather than letting fees and penalties accumulate.
