Automated WhatsApp Court Hearing Reminders for Advocates and Clients

WhatsApp is where your juniors and clients already are. Here is how to send hearing reminders through it properly — with timing, consent and confidentiality in mind.

Executive summary

Almost every chamber in India already uses WhatsApp for hearing dates — usually as a forwarded photo of the diary page or a typed message from a clerk late at night. It works until the clerk is on leave, the message goes to the wrong group, or a client's number changes.

Automated reminders keep the habit but remove the person in the middle. Once a hearing is confirmed in the diary, a reminder goes out on a fixed schedule — two days before and on the morning of the hearing — to the advocate and the team assigned to it. Client updates can be shared separately and deliberately.

This guide covers how such reminders work through the WhatsApp Business API, what consent you need, what a good reminder says, and what should never be in one.

Setting up hearing reminders that people actually read

  1. Start from confirmed dates only

    A reminder is only as good as the date behind it. Send reminders only for hearings that an advocate has confirmed from the order. In Legal Staff, dates picked out of an order stay unconfirmed — and send nothing — until someone checks them.

  2. Decide who gets which reminder

    The advocate appearing, the junior preparing the file and the clerk who carries it need the internal reminder. The client needs a different, shorter message — and only when you choose to send it.

  3. Collect consent and correct numbers

    Ask clients for the WhatsApp number they want updates on and record that they agreed to receive them. Business messaging rules expect recipients to have opted in, and it is good practice anyway.

  4. Use a fixed schedule

    Two days before gives time to pull the file, prepare a note or arrange a proxy; the morning reminder catches anything forgotten. More than that and people start ignoring them.

  5. Keep messages short and factual

    Case title or your internal reference, court and court hall or bench, date, item number if known, and purpose. No legal advice, no strategy and no sensitive facts.

  6. Connect the WhatsApp Business API

    Automated reminders from a business number go through the WhatsApp Business API, which uses pre-approved message templates for business-initiated messages. Legal Staff sends reminders through it once your chamber's number is connected; until then, email reminders work.

  7. Review delivery and replies

    Look at which reminders were delivered and whether a client replied with a question. Replies need a human answer; an automated reminder is not a conversation.

Personal WhatsApp versus the WhatsApp Business API

A clerk typing messages from a personal phone is not automation. It depends on one person, mixes client conversations with personal chats and leaves no record in the case file. Broadcast lists on the regular app help a little, but they still need someone to type every message.

The WhatsApp Business API is the route for automated messages from a business. It requires a verified business number, and messages the business starts — like a reminder — use templates approved in advance. That is why a reminder reads the same way each time, with the case details filled in. Template approval and opt-in rules are set by WhatsApp, so check their current policy when you set up.

Once connected, reminders run from the diary. When a date changes, the reminder changes with it — something a forwarded diary photo can never do.

What not to put in a WhatsApp reminder

Advocates owe clients a duty of confidentiality. A reminder can be seen on a lock screen, forwarded, or read by a family member. Keep it to logistics.

  • No description of the allegations, the evidence or the strategy.
  • No opposing party details beyond what is needed to identify the case.
  • No fee demands mixed with hearing reminders — send invoices separately.
  • For sensitive matters (matrimonial, criminal, POCSO-related), use an internal reference rather than the case title.

Writing client updates that reduce phone calls

Most calls to a chamber are some version of 'what happened today?' and 'when is the next date?'. A short, consistent update after each hearing answers both before the client has to ask. It also creates a record that the client was told — useful when a client later says they never knew a date or a direction.

Keep client updates separate from internal reminders. The internal reminder tells a junior to pull the file and file the reply; the client update tells the client that the matter was adjourned, the next date, and whether they need to attend or send documents. In Legal Staff, client updates are shared deliberately rather than sent automatically for every change, so the advocate decides what the client sees.

A good update avoids legal jargon. 'Renotified' or 'S.O.' means nothing to most clients; 'the court did not take up the case today and will give a new date' does.

  • Say what happened in one line.
  • Give the next date, court and whether the client must attend.
  • List anything the client must do or send, with a date.
  • Invite questions by call or meeting rather than long WhatsApp threads about the merits.

Common pitfalls

Reminding from unconfirmed dates

If a misread date triggers a reminder, the client turns up on the wrong day. Only confirmed dates should send anything.

Forgetting to update reminders after a change

When a matter is renotified or a date is advanced, the old reminder must be cancelled. Reminders driven from the diary handle this; manual messages do not.

Sending to group chats

Client families often ask to be added to a group. Groups make it hard to control who sees what. Send to the named client number they agreed to.

Too many messages

A reminder every day for a week trains people to mute you. Two reminders per hearing is enough for most matters.

No email fallback

Some clients and in-house teams prefer email or do not use WhatsApp for work. Keep email reminders available.

Frequently asked questions

Can I send automated reminders from my personal WhatsApp number?

Not reliably. Automated business messages go through the WhatsApp Business API, which needs a business number. Legal Staff sends reminders through it once your chamber's number is connected, and email reminders work in the meantime.

When should hearing reminders go out?

A practical default is two days before the hearing and on the morning of the hearing. That gives time to prepare and catches last-minute misses without flooding people with messages.

Should clients get the same reminders as the team?

Usually not. The team needs internal detail such as pending tasks; the client needs a short note of the date, court and anything they must bring or do. Share client updates deliberately.

Is it safe to share hearing details on WhatsApp?

Logistics such as date, court and purpose are generally fine with the client's consent. Keep facts of the case, evidence and advice out of reminders, and use internal references for sensitive matters.

General information for advocates, not legal advice. Statutes, rules and court practice directions change; check the bare Act, the latest amendments and your court's rules before relying on any date or section.
