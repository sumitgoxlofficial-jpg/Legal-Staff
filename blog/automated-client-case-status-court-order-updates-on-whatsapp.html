Sending Clients Case Status and Court Order Updates on WhatsApp, Professionally and Safely

Clients want to know what happened in court today. A structured WhatsApp update, sent with consent and reviewed by an advocate, answers that without a dozen phone calls.

Executive summary

The most common client question is simple: what happened today, and when is the next date? Answering it by phone, one client at a time, takes hours each week. A short written update after each hearing, with the next date and any action the client must take, is faster and leaves a record.

WhatsApp is where most Indian clients already are, but using it for case updates raises fair questions: consent to be messaged, confidentiality of what is sent, who in the chamber may send what, and the line between keeping a client informed and soliciting work, which the Bar Council of India Rules restrict.

This guide sets out a workflow that keeps updates useful and professional: get consent, send a fixed structure, have an advocate approve anything that describes the order, and never use the channel to advertise. It also explains what Legal Staff does today and what depends on connecting the WhatsApp Business API.

A workflow for client updates

  1. Take consent at engagement

    Ask in the engagement or fee letter whether the client agrees to receive case updates on WhatsApp, on which number, and who else (for example a company’s legal manager) may receive them. Record it on the client record.

  2. Agree what will be shared

    Decide with the client what updates contain: hearing outcome, next date, what they need to do. For sensitive matters, agree to send a short note on WhatsApp and share details through a more controlled channel.

  3. Use a fixed structure

    Matter name, court, date, what happened in one or two lines, the next date, and any action required from the client. A fixed format reduces mistakes and makes updates easy to review.

  4. Have an advocate approve the description of the order

    A junior can prepare the update, but the description of what the court did should be approved by an advocate who has read the order. Do not forward a draft summary as the court’s view.

  5. Send after the order or record is available

    Where possible, check the next date against the uploaded order before telling the client. Corridor information about dates is sometimes wrong.

  6. Keep the record on the matter

    Log what was sent, when and to whom, on the matter itself, so anyone in the chamber can see what the client has been told.

Informing a client is not soliciting

The Bar Council of India Rules prohibit advocates from soliciting work or advertising, directly or indirectly, with limited exceptions such as the information that may appear on an advocate’s website. Updating an existing client about their own matter is ordinary professional communication, not advertising.

The line is crossed when the same channel is used for promotional broadcasts: success stories, offers, newsletters sent to people who are not clients, or messages designed to attract new work. Keep client updates strictly about the client’s own matter, and do not add marketing footers.

Writing an update the client can act on

A good update is short and specific. For example: Matter: recovery suit against the supplier, District Court. Today: the defendant sought time to file its reply; the court allowed two weeks on costs. Next date: 12 December. Action for you: please send the original delivery challans by 1 December. That is enough for a client to plan, and it avoids characterising the court’s reasons, which belongs in a fuller discussion.

Avoid predictions in written updates. Phrases like we will win or this will be decided next time create expectations the chamber cannot control. If the client needs advice on prospects, give it in a meeting or a considered note, not in a message sent from the corridor. Where a matter is sensitive, a neutral line such as there is an update on your matter, please call when convenient is often the safest message.

Common pitfalls

Messaging without consent

Do not add clients to updates without their agreement, and use only the number they gave for this purpose.

Sending to group chats

Client matters should not be discussed in groups that include people who are not the client’s authorised representatives.

Forwarding unchecked summaries

An automated summary is a draft. Have an advocate check it before it describes the court’s order to a client.

Promotional content in updates

Adding offers, success stories or newsletters to case updates moves them towards advertising, which the Bar Council rules restrict.

Sensitive detail on a shared phone

In family, criminal or employment matters, the client’s phone may be seen by others. Agree a discreet format.

Frequently asked questions

Is it allowed for advocates to send case updates on WhatsApp?

Updating your own client about their own matter is ordinary professional communication. Take consent, keep it confidential and do not use the channel for promotional messages, which the Bar Council of India Rules restrict.

Do I need the WhatsApp Business API for automated updates?

For automated, business-initiated messages at volume, yes. The WhatsApp Business Platform requires recipient opt-in and approved message templates. Legal Staff sends WhatsApp messages once your chamber has connected the Business API.

Should the full court order be sent on WhatsApp?

It is usually better to send a short update and share the order through a controlled channel such as a client portal, especially in sensitive matters.

Who should approve the update?

An advocate who has read the order should approve any description of what the court did. Juniors and clerks can prepare the update.
