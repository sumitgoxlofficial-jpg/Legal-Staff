Managing Junior Advocates and Court Clerks: A Daily Board, Clear Tasks and Fewer Missed Dates

Most missed dates in a busy chamber are hand-off failures, not legal mistakes. A daily hearing board, named tasks and a fixed after-court routine fix most of them.

Executive summary

A litigation chamber runs on coordination. The senior may be in one court, two juniors in others, and a clerk shuttling between filing counters and the copying section. When information lives in one paper diary or one WhatsApp group, the chamber depends on the memory of whoever was in court that day.

The fix is a routine more than a product: an evening board of tomorrow’s matters, a morning check for clashes, named owners for each task with a due date, and an after-court update of the order and next date on the matter itself. Software helps because it makes the routine the default and shows the senior what is pending without asking.

This guide sets out a daily rhythm that works for chambers of three to thirty people, the tasks to delegate to juniors versus clerks, and how Legal Staff’s hearing board, task assignment and reminders support it.

A daily rhythm for the chamber

  1. Prepare tomorrow’s board in the evening

    Before leaving, the clerk or a designated junior checks the cause lists for the next day and confirms each listed matter against the diary. Unlisted matters expected the next day are flagged so someone checks again in the morning.

  2. Check for clashes and assign appearances

    Where the senior is listed in two courts at the same time, decide the night before who appears where, who will mention or seek a passover, and who carries which file. Record the reassignment on the hearing, not in a message.

  3. Send a morning briefing

    A short morning message listing each person’s matters, courts, item numbers where known and the papers they need saves calls in the corridor. It should go out before people leave for court.

  4. Give every task one owner and one due date

    Draft the reply, file the vakalatnama, apply for the certified copy, collect the copy, inspect the file: each is a task with a named owner and date. A task owned by everyone is owned by no one.

  5. Update orders and next dates after court

    Whoever appeared records the outcome, the next date and any directions the same day, and uploads the order when it becomes available. The next date only goes into the diary once checked against the order.

  6. Review pending tasks weekly

    Once a week the senior or a senior associate reviews overdue tasks, upcoming limitation dates and matters with no next date. Matters without a next date are where cases quietly fall off the board.

Delegation without losing control

Seniors often hesitate to delegate because they lose sight of what has been done. A shared board solves that: the senior can see which tasks are open, who owns them and whether the next date has been recorded, without a phone call. Juniors gain clarity on what is expected and when, which matters for their training as much as for the chamber.

Court clerks are often the chamber’s memory for filing counters, registry practice and copying sections. Some High Courts register advocates’ clerks under their own rules, so check what your court requires for a clerk to file or inspect on your behalf. Give clerks a simple view: their tasks for today, with the matter, court and documents needed.

  • Record reassignments on the hearing itself so the diary stays accurate.
  • Keep drafting tasks tied to versions, so the senior reviews the right draft.
  • Use roles so clerks see what they need without access to every client document.

How Legal Staff supports the routine

Legal Staff gives the chamber a shared court diary across all courts with clash detection, a hearing board, task assignment with due dates, and the ability to reassign a hearing to a junior. A daily morning briefing and WhatsApp and email reminders (two days before and on the morning of a hearing) go to the advocate and team once WhatsApp is connected.

After court, an order can be pasted or uploaded; Legal Staff summarises it with paragraph references and picks out the next date, which goes into the diary only after an advocate confirms it. It does not monitor cause lists for you, so the evening board check still matters; Legal Staff makes the result visible to everyone.

Training juniors through the board

A shared board is also a training tool. A junior who can see the whole chamber’s week learns how matters move: which applications are moved before which stage, how long a drafting task usually takes, and why a senior asks for a certified copy on the day of judgment. Pair each new junior with a senior associate for the first month and let them prepare the evening board under supervision.

Feedback is most useful when it is tied to the task. When a senior reviews a draft, the comments and the corrected version should sit on the same matter, so the junior can compare versions later. Over a year, the chamber builds a record of how its own work is done, which makes the next junior easier to train and protects the practice when someone leaves.

Common pitfalls

One person’s diary as the master record

When the only complete diary belongs to one clerk or one junior, illness or a resignation becomes a crisis. Keep the master record shared.

Instructions only on WhatsApp groups

Group messages scroll away and cannot be tracked to completion. Put the task on the matter and use messages for reminders.

Next date recorded from memory

Next dates should be checked against the order or the court’s record, not taken from the corridor conversation.

No review of matters without a next date

A weekly check of matters that have no upcoming date catches cases adjourned sine die, reserved or lost in transfer.

Everyone sees everything

Not every team member needs access to every client file. Set roles to match the work.

Frequently asked questions

How do I assign a hearing to a junior in Legal Staff?

Open the hearing in the diary and reassign it to a team member. The assignee sees it on their board and receives the reminders for it.

Can court clerks use Legal Staff?

Yes, as team members with a role you choose. They can see their assigned tasks and hearings. Legal Staff is a web app that works in mobile browsers; there is no separate native app.

Does Legal Staff check the cause list automatically?

No. Legal Staff does not scrape court cause lists. Next dates come from the orders you add and confirm, and where a court-data provider is connected, cases can be imported by CNR.

What is the most useful single habit for a chamber?

Recording the outcome and next date on the matter the same day as the hearing, checked against the order. Most other problems follow from skipping it.

General information for advocates, not legal advice. Statutes, rules and court practice directions change; check the bare Act, the latest amendments and your court's rules before relying on any date or section.
