DIR-3 KYC Web vs e-Form: Reactivating a Deactivated DIN Quickly

A missed DIR-3 KYC deactivates the DIN and blocks every filing that director signs. Here is how to choose between the web version and the e-form, pay the ₹5,000 fee and get back to work.

Executive summary

Every individual who holds a Director Identification Number (DIN) must submit KYC details to the MCA under rule 12A of the Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules, 2014. When it is not filed by the due date, the MCA marks the DIN as deactivated, and the director cannot sign or be named in most MCA filings until the KYC is completed with the late fee. For a startup with two directors, one deactivated DIN can stall an INC-20A, an AOC-4 or a change in directors.

There are two ways to file: e-form DIR-3 KYC, which is a full form with DSC and professional certification, and DIR-3 KYC-WEB, a shorter web service that verifies details already on the MCA record through OTPs. Which one you can use depends on whether you have filed the full e-form before and whether your contact details have changed.

The reactivation itself is usually straightforward: file the right version with the ₹5,000 fee and the DIN is reactivated once the filing is accepted. The due date and frequency of KYC have been the subject of MCA review; check the current text of rule 12A on the MCA portal before planning your next filing.

Reactivating a deactivated DIN, step by step

  1. Confirm why the DIN is deactivated

    Check the DIN status on the MCA V3 portal. Deactivation for non-filing of DIR-3 KYC is different from disqualification under section 164(2), which follows non-filing of financial statements or annual returns by a company for three consecutive financial years. KYC deactivation is cured by filing KYC; disqualification is not.

  2. Decide between the web version and the e-form

    Use DIR-3 KYC-WEB if you have previously filed the full e-form DIR-3 KYC and your personal mobile number and email address on record are unchanged. Use e-form DIR-3 KYC if you have never filed the e-form, or if you need to update mobile, email or other details. If in doubt, the e-form always works.

  3. Check your mobile and email are your own

    Both versions verify through OTPs sent to the director's personal mobile and email. The MCA expects these to be unique to the individual, so a shared office email or the company secretary's phone number will cause problems. Update them through the e-form if they are not yours.

  4. Prepare documents for the e-form

    For the e-form, keep the PAN, Aadhaar (where allotted), proof of address and, for foreign nationals, the passport, which is mandatory for them. The form is signed with the director's own valid DSC and certified by a practising Chartered Accountant, Company Secretary or Cost Accountant.

  5. Pay the ₹5,000 late fee

    After the due date, the MCA charges a fee of ₹5,000 for the KYC filing for each DIN. The fee is the same whichever version you use. Pay it on the portal; it is generated automatically for a deactivated DIN.

  6. Verify reactivation and resume filings

    Once the filing is processed, the DIN status should show as active. Check it before you sign the next form. Then file any company forms that were held up, starting with those carrying daily additional fees or penalties, such as INC-20A or the annual returns.

Annual, or once every three years?

For several years, rule 12A has required every DIN holder to file KYC annually by 30 September. The MCA has since reviewed this frequency, including a move towards KYC once every three years with an obligation to update contact details whenever they change. Because the effective date and exact wording of any change matter, we do not state a frequency as settled here.

Before you plan the next cycle, read the current rule 12A on the MCA portal or ask the professional who certifies your KYC. If a change has taken effect, note when your next KYC is due and whether a change in mobile, email or address triggers a filing within a fixed number of days.

Directors who also hold LLP designations

A DIN also serves as the Designated Partner Identification Number for LLPs. A deactivated DIN therefore blocks LLP filings as well, including Form 8 and Form 11. If you sit on the board of a company and are a designated partner in an LLP, one KYC filing covers you, but the deactivation affects both.

Common pitfalls

Using the web version after changing your phone number

The web version only confirms existing details. If your mobile or email has changed, file the e-form.

Using the company email or a shared number

OTPs go to the director personally. Shared or office contact details often clash with another DIN holder's record.

An expired DSC

The e-form needs the director's own valid DSC. Renew it before you start, especially for foreign directors who need longer lead time.

Confusing deactivation with disqualification

Filing KYC reactivates a DIN deactivated for non-filing of KYC. It does nothing for a section 164(2) disqualification.

Waiting until the last week of September

Portal load near the due date is a known issue. File in the first half of the window.

Frequently asked questions

How long does DIN reactivation take after filing DIR-3 KYC?

Once the KYC filing with the ₹5,000 fee is accepted, the DIN is usually shown as active again without a separate application. Timing depends on portal processing, so check the DIN status before signing any other form.

Can I use DIR-3 KYC-WEB if my DIN is deactivated?

Generally yes, if you have filed the full e-form DIR-3 KYC in an earlier year and your personal mobile and email are unchanged. The ₹5,000 fee applies either way. If your details changed, use the e-form.

Is DIR-3 KYC needed for a director with no active company?

The requirement is attached to holding a DIN, not to an active directorship. If you hold a DIN, file the KYC as rule 12A requires, or consider surrendering the DIN through the prescribed process if you no longer need it.

Does a foreign director need to file DIR-3 KYC?

Yes, if they hold a DIN. For foreign nationals the passport is mandatory, and documents executed abroad typically need notarisation and apostille or consular attestation, so start early.
