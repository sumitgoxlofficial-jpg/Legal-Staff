Cost Records (CRA-1) vs Cost Audit (CRA-2/CRA-3/CRA-4): Turnover Thresholds

Cost records and cost audit are separate tests with separate thresholds. Here is how to check both, and the CRA forms and dates that follow.

Executive summary

Section 148 of the Companies Act, 2013 lets the Central Government require specified companies to keep cost records and, above higher thresholds, to have those records audited. The detail is in the Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules, 2014, which list the covered industries in two tables: Table A for regulated sectors and Table B for non-regulated sectors, each mapped to customs tariff or service codes.

The first test is cost records. A company producing goods or providing services listed in the rules must maintain cost records in Form CRA-1 if its overall turnover from all products and services in the immediately preceding financial year was ₹35 crore or more. The second test is cost audit: for Table A companies, overall turnover of ₹50 crore or more and turnover of ₹25 crore or more from the individual covered product or service; for Table B, ₹100 crore and ₹35 crore respectively.

If cost audit applies, the board appoints a cost accountant in practice within 180 days of the start of the financial year, intimates the appointment in CRA-2, the auditor reports in CRA-3, and the company files the report in CRA-4 within 30 days of receiving it. The thresholds and exemptions have been amended before, so check the current text of the rules against your latest audited numbers each year.

Step-by-step: checking applicability and staying compliant

  1. Map your products and services to the tables

    Identify the customs tariff headings (for goods) or service descriptions that apply to your revenue lines and check whether they appear in Table A or Table B of rule 3. If none of your activities is covered, the rules do not apply.

  2. Test the cost-records threshold

    Take overall turnover from all products and services for the immediately preceding financial year. At ₹35 crore or more, with a covered activity, cost records in CRA-1 are required for the current year.

  3. Check the exemptions

    Micro and small enterprises classified under the MSMED Act are excluded from the cost-records requirement. For cost audit, the rules also exclude companies whose foreign-exchange export revenue exceeds 75% of total revenue and companies operating from a special economic zone, among others. Read the current exemption text.

  4. Test the cost-audit threshold

    For Table A: overall turnover ₹50 crore or more and the individual covered product or service ₹25 crore or more. For Table B: ₹100 crore or more overall and ₹35 crore or more for the individual product or service.

  5. Appoint the cost auditor and file CRA-2

    The board appoints a cost accountant in practice (not the statutory auditor) within 180 days of the start of the financial year, with remuneration later ratified by shareholders. File CRA-2 within 30 days of the board meeting or within 180 days of the start of the year, whichever is earlier.

  6. Receive CRA-3 and file CRA-4

    The cost auditor submits the report in CRA-3 to the board within 180 days of the end of the financial year. The company files it with the Central Government in CRA-4 within 30 days of receipt, with the board's explanations on any qualifications.

Cost records are not the same as cost audit

Many companies discover the cost-records requirement late because they assume it only matters once cost audit applies. In fact a company can cross the ₹35 crore cost-records threshold years before it needs a cost audit, and the records must still be kept in the CRA-1 format — material, employee cost, overheads, by product or service, reconciled with the financial accounts.

Keeping cost records properly also prepares you for the audit: when turnover crosses the audit thresholds, a year of clean records makes the first cost audit much easier.

  • "Turnover" for these tests is the overall turnover from all products and services in the immediately preceding financial year, not only the covered product.
  • Turnover crossing a threshold in one year makes the requirement apply to the following year.
  • The cost auditor must be independent of the statutory audit — the statutory auditor cannot also be the cost auditor.

What a cost auditor will ask for

Expect requests for production and sales quantities, consumption records, a product-wise cost sheet, a reconciliation of cost and financial profit, details of related-party transactions and any captive consumption. If you also need CAS-4 certificates for GST valuation, the same cost data supports them.

Common pitfalls

Testing on the wrong year's turnover

Applicability is based on the immediately preceding financial year. Use audited figures and re-test every year.

Missing the 180-day appointment window

If the cost auditor is not appointed within 180 days of the start of the year, both the appointment and CRA-2 become late.

Assuming exemptions without reading them

MSE and export-oriented exemptions have conditions. Document why you rely on one.

Appointing the statutory auditor

The statutory auditor cannot act as cost auditor. Appoint a separate cost accountant in practice.

Not ratifying remuneration

Cost-auditor remuneration fixed by the board must be ratified by shareholders. Put it on the AGM or EGM agenda.

Frequently asked questions

What is the turnover limit for maintaining cost records?

A company engaged in a product or service listed in the rules must maintain cost records if its overall turnover from all products and services in the immediately preceding financial year was ₹35 crore or more, unless an exemption applies.

When does cost audit become mandatory?

For regulated sectors (Table A), when overall turnover is ₹50 crore or more and the individual covered product or service is ₹25 crore or more. For non-regulated sectors (Table B), the limits are ₹100 crore and ₹35 crore respectively, based on the preceding year.

Is Form CRA-1 filed with MCA?

No. CRA-1 is the format in which cost records are maintained. CRA-2 (appointment of cost auditor) and CRA-4 (cost audit report) are the forms filed with MCA.

Who can be appointed as cost auditor?

A cost accountant in practice or a firm of cost accountants. The company's statutory auditor cannot be appointed as its cost auditor.
