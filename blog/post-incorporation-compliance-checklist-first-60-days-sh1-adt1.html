First 60 Days After Incorporation: SH-1 Share Certificates, Stamp Duty & ADT-1

The certificate of incorporation is the start, not the finish. These are the board, auditor, share-certificate, stamp-duty and register tasks a new company should close in its first two months.

Executive summary

The first two months after incorporation set the tone for every later filing, funding round and due diligence. The Companies Act, 2013 packs several hard deadlines into this period: the first board meeting within 30 days, the first auditor within 30 days, share certificates to subscribers within two months, and the INC-20A commencement declaration within 180 days. None of them are optional for a private limited company, and several leave a paper trail that investors will ask for.

This checklist covers each task in the order we recommend doing it, with the section that requires it, and the document you should have on file at the end. It also explains stamp duty on the issue of shares after the 2020 amendments to the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, which many founders miss because it is not collected through the MCA portal.

Treat this as a working list for a typical private limited company incorporated through SPICe+. Companies with foreign shareholders, regulated businesses or public companies have extra steps, and some rules (especially stamp duty mechanics, which depend on the state) should be confirmed with a professional for your facts.

Your first-60-days compliance sequence

  1. Open the bank account and collect subscription money

    Use the bank account opened through SPICe+ (AGILE-PRO-S) or open one with the incorporation documents, PAN and board authority. Get each subscriber to pay the full amount for their shares into this account. The bank statement becomes the core attachment for INC-20A and the basis for issuing share certificates.

  2. Hold the first board meeting within 30 days

    Section 173(1) requires the first board meeting within 30 days of incorporation. Typical business: take note of the certificate of incorporation, MoA and AoA; record directors' disclosures of interest in Form MBP-1 (section 184) and non-disqualification declarations in Form DIR-8 (section 164); appoint the first auditor; approve opening of the bank account and authorised signatories; approve issue of share certificates; fix the registered office records; and authorise filing of INC-20A. Minute it properly and sign the minutes within the time allowed by Secretarial Standard SS-1.

  3. Appoint the first auditor within 30 days

    Under section 139(6) the Board appoints the first auditor within 30 days of registration. If the Board does not, the members must appoint one at an extraordinary general meeting within 90 days. Get the auditor's written consent and eligibility certificate under section 139(1) before the meeting. The first auditor holds office until the conclusion of the first annual general meeting.

  4. Decide on ADT-1 for the first auditor

    Form ADT-1 is clearly required within 15 days of the meeting where an auditor is appointed under section 139(1), usually at the AGM. Whether it is mandatory for the first auditor appointed by the Board under section 139(6) has been debated, and many professionals file it anyway as a matter of caution. Ask your adviser which approach your ROC expects; filing it costs little and avoids a question later.

  5. Issue SH-1 share certificates within two months

    Section 56(4)(a) requires certificates for subscribers to the memorandum to be delivered within two months of incorporation. Prepare them in Form SH-1 under rule 5 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014, signed by two directors (or a director and the company secretary) and with the common seal if the company has one. Enter the details in the register of members.

  6. Pay stamp duty on the issue of shares

    Since 1 July 2020, stamp duty on the issue of securities under the amended Indian Stamp Act is 0.005% of the value. For shares issued in physical form, the issuing company pays it to the state government, generally where its registered office is located, using that state's e-stamping or e-challan system. Keep the payment proof with the certificates. Confirm the value base and payment mode for your state before paying.

  7. Set up statutory registers

    Start the register of members (section 88, Form MGT-1), register of directors and key managerial personnel (section 170), register of contracts in which directors are interested (section 189, Form MBP-4) and register of charges (section 85, Form CHG-7). Keep them at the registered office, physically or electronically as the rules permit.

  8. Lock in INC-20A and other registrations

    With money received and certificates issued, file INC-20A well before the 180-day limit; there is no benefit in waiting. Also check GST registration (if your turnover or business type requires it), professional tax and shops and establishments registration in your state, and EPFO/ESIC once employee thresholds apply.

Physical certificates or demat?

Rule 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014, notified in 2023, requires private companies that are not small companies to issue securities only in dematerialised form and to facilitate dematerialisation of existing securities, with a transition period. Small companies were excluded when the rule was notified. If your company is, or will become, a non-small private company, plan for an ISIN, a depository connection and a registrar and transfer agent rather than paper certificates. Check the current definition of a small company and the rule's current text before deciding.

Where shares are held in demat form, stamp duty on issue is collected through the depository rather than paid by the company directly, which is one less step to track.

What your company file should contain at day 60

Investors, banks and auditors will ask for these documents repeatedly. Keeping them in one place from the start saves weeks in your first due diligence.

  • Certificate of incorporation, MoA and AoA, PAN and TAN
  • Signed minutes of the first board meeting with attendance
  • MBP-1 and DIR-8 from every director
  • Auditor consent, eligibility certificate, appointment letter and ADT-1 receipt (if filed)
  • Bank statement showing subscription money received
  • Copies of SH-1 certificates with stamp duty payment proof
  • Register of members and register of directors and KMP
  • INC-20A filing receipt (or a dated plan to file it)

Common pitfalls

Skipping the first auditor

It is the most common year-1 miss. Without an auditor, the first financial statements cannot be audited and the AOC-4 timeline slips.

Issuing certificates before money is received

Certificates should reflect fully paid shares. Collect subscription money first, then issue.

Forgetting stamp duty

Stamp duty on issue is not collected through SPICe+ for physical certificates. Unstamped certificates can create problems in later transfers and diligence.

Minutes that are written months later

Back-dated or reconstructed minutes are easy to spot. Write and sign them promptly, following Secretarial Standard SS-1.

Treating INC-20A as a 180-day problem

Waiting until day 170 leaves no margin for a deactivated DIN or a missing bank statement. File as soon as subscription money is in.

Frequently asked questions

Is ADT-1 required for the first auditor?

The law clearly requires ADT-1 for auditors appointed under section 139(1), typically at the AGM. For the first auditor appointed by the Board under section 139(6), practice differs, and many professionals file ADT-1 anyway as a precaution. Confirm the approach with your adviser; filing it is low-cost.

What is the stamp duty on share certificates?

Since 1 July 2020, stamp duty on the issue of shares under the amended Indian Stamp Act is 0.005% of the value. For physical shares the company pays it to the state, generally where the registered office is. Confirm the value base and payment method for your state.

Who signs an SH-1 share certificate?

Under rule 5 of the Share Capital and Debentures Rules, a certificate is signed by two directors, or by a director and the company secretary where one is appointed, and carries the common seal if the company has one. Details are entered in the register of members.

What happens if the first board meeting is late?

Section 173(4) provides a penalty for each officer whose duty it was to give notice of the meeting and who failed to do so. Hold the meeting as soon as possible, minute it accurately, and do not back-date it.

General information, not legal or tax advice. Government fees, stamp duty and due dates are revised from time to time; confirm the current figures on the MCA, state stamp and Income Tax portals or with a practising professional before filing.
