How to Get a Certified CMA Project Report for MSME Bank & CGTMSE Loans

Bank "CMA data" is a six-form credit monitoring statement, not a cost audit. Here is what goes into it, how lenders test it, and how to make it hold up.

Executive summary

When a bank asks an MSME for "CMA data", it means the Credit Monitoring Arrangement statements — a standard set of historical and projected financials that lenders use to assess working-capital and term-loan proposals. The format is usually six forms: existing and proposed limits, an operating statement, an analysis of the balance sheet, a comparative statement of current assets and current liabilities, the computation of maximum permissible bank finance, and a fund-flow statement.

A project report wraps this data in a narrative: the business, promoters, project cost and means of finance, capacity and assumptions, and the key ratios — debt service coverage ratio (DSCR), current ratio, debt-equity and interest coverage. Lenders compare your projections with your history and with the industry; aggressive, unexplained jumps are the most common reason a proposal stalls.

For collateral-free loans to micro and small enterprises, banks can seek a guarantee from the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE). The guarantee ceiling and fee have been revised several times — the ceiling went up to ₹5 crore in 2023 and a further increase to ₹10 crore was announced in the Union Budget 2025-26 — so confirm the current limit, coverage and fee with your lender.

Step-by-step: preparing CMA data and a project report

  1. Collect three years of history

    Audited financial statements for the last two or three years, the current year's provisional figures, GST returns, bank statements and the stock and debtor statements submitted to existing lenders. Projections are only credible if they start from reconciled actuals.

  2. Set out the project and the funding ask

    Describe the expansion or new project, the cost (land, building, plant, pre-operative expenses, margin for working capital) and the means of finance — promoter contribution, term loan, working-capital limit, any subsidy.

  3. Build assumptions you can defend

    Capacity utilisation, selling prices, raw-material costs, credit periods for debtors and creditors, and inventory holding. Tie each to evidence: orders, quotations, past performance or industry data.

  4. Prepare Forms I to VI

    Populate the six CMA forms for the past years, the current estimate and the projected years (often the loan tenure). Make sure the balance sheet balances every year and that the cash flow ties to it.

  5. Compute and stress-test the ratios

    Calculate DSCR (average and year-wise), current ratio, TOL/TNW, debt-equity and interest coverage. Run a downside case — lower sales or margins — to show the loan is still serviceable.

  6. Get it reviewed and certified

    Have a practising professional review the report for consistency and certify it in the bank's format with a UDIN. Many lenders accept reports certified by a practising CA or cost accountant; check the bank's requirement.

  7. Prepare the CGTMSE and Udyam papers

    Keep your Udyam registration current, as eligibility for MSE schemes depends on it. Ask the bank whether the proposal will be covered under CGTMSE and what the annual guarantee fee will be.

How banks size working-capital limits

Two methods are common. For smaller limits, many banks use the turnover method associated with the Nayak Committee: working-capital requirement taken as 25% of projected annual turnover, of which the bank finances 20% and the borrower brings 5% as margin. For larger limits, banks assess the working-capital gap and apply a margin — the "second method of lending" from the Tandon Committee requires the borrower to fund 25% of total current assets from long-term sources.

Your Form IV and Form V must support the limit you ask for. If your projected debtor days are far shorter than your history, explain why — a new customer contract, factoring or a change in terms.

DSCR and the other ratios

DSCR measures whether cash from operations covers the loan instalments and interest. A common form is (profit after tax + depreciation + interest on term loan) ÷ (term-loan instalment + interest on term loan) for each year. Lenders look at both the average and the weakest year.

There is no single statutory benchmark; each bank sets its own. Treat any DSCR close to 1 as a red flag and show how a cushion is maintained in a downside case.

  • Current ratio: current assets ÷ current liabilities, including bank borrowings for working capital.
  • TOL/TNW: total outside liabilities ÷ tangible net worth — a leverage check.
  • Interest coverage: EBITDA ÷ interest — how comfortably profits cover interest.

Common pitfalls

Projections that do not match history

Doubling sales in year one without orders or capacity evidence is the fastest way to lose a credit officer's confidence.

Balance sheet that does not balance

A mismatch in any projected year signals a broken model. Reconcile every year before submitting.

Ignoring GST return figures

Lenders compare reported sales with GST returns and bank credits. Explain any differences.

Uncertified or unsigned reports

A report without the professional's signature, membership number and UDIN may be returned. Ask the bank what certification it needs.

Assuming CGTMSE cover is automatic

Coverage is decided by the lender within the scheme rules and carries an annual fee. Ask early.

Frequently asked questions

Is a bank "CMA report" the same as a Cost and Management Accountant report?

No. In lending, CMA stands for Credit Monitoring Arrangement — a standard set of financial statements. It can be prepared or certified by a practising chartered accountant or cost accountant, depending on what your bank accepts.

How many years of projections are needed?

Banks usually want projections for the current year and future years — often covering the full tenure of a term loan, and at least one to two years for a working-capital limit. Ask your bank for its template.

What is the CGTMSE limit for collateral-free loans?

The CGTMSE guarantee ceiling has been raised over time — to ₹5 crore in 2023, with a further increase to ₹10 crore announced in the Union Budget 2025-26. Coverage percentages and the annual guarantee fee depend on the loan size and borrower category, so confirm the current terms with your lender.

What DSCR do banks expect?

There is no fixed statutory number; each lender sets its own benchmark by sector and loan type. A DSCR that stays comfortably above 1 in every projected year, including a downside case, is what makes a proposal credible.
