Converting a Proprietorship to a Pvt Ltd Without Losing GST ITC via Form ITC-02

A proprietorship cannot simply "become" a company. Here is how to transfer the business as a going concern, carry the GST credit across with ITC-02 and keep the move tax-neutral.

Executive summary

There is no form that converts a sole proprietorship into a company. Instead, you incorporate a new private limited company and transfer the proprietorship's business to it as a going concern under a business transfer agreement (BTA), usually in exchange for shares. The company is a new legal person with its own PAN, so it needs its own GST registration — GST registration follows the PAN.

The unutilised input tax credit in the proprietor's electronic credit ledger is not lost. Section 18(3) of the CGST Act allows it to be transferred to the transferee when a business is transferred with a specific provision for the transfer of liabilities, and rule 41 of the CGST Rules prescribes Form GST ITC-02 for this, backed by a certificate from a practising chartered accountant or cost accountant.

On the income-tax side, the transfer can be made tax-neutral if the conditions historically in section 47(xiv) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 are met: all business assets and liabilities pass to the company, the proprietor holds at least 50% of the voting power for five years, and receives no consideration other than shares. The Income-tax Act, 2025 has applied from 1 April 2026 with renumbered provisions, so confirm the current section reference and conditions with your tax adviser before signing the BTA.

Step-by-step: proprietorship to private limited, with ITC intact

  1. Plan the cut-over date

    Choose a transfer date — often the start of a month or quarter — so that stock, debtors, creditors and the credit ledger can be closed cleanly. Bring GST returns of the proprietorship up to date before that date.

  2. Incorporate the company

    Incorporate a private limited company through SPICe+, with the proprietor as a director and the main shareholder. Include objects that cover the existing business. Apply for the company's GSTIN through AGILE-PRO-S or separately.

  3. Sign a business transfer agreement

    Transfer the business as a going concern — assets, liabilities, contracts, employees and licences — with a specific clause that the company takes over the liabilities. Record the consideration as an allotment of shares. Pay stamp duty as applicable in your state.

  4. Allot shares to the proprietor

    The board allots shares to the proprietor as consideration for the business, and the company files the return of allotment (PAS-3). Keep the shareholding at or above the level needed for tax neutrality.

  5. Get the professional certificate and file ITC-02

    A practising CA or cost accountant certifies that the transfer was made with a specific provision for the transfer of liabilities. The proprietor (transferor) files ITC-02 on the GST portal from the old GSTIN, naming the company's GSTIN and the amounts of CGST, SGST/UTGST and IGST to be transferred.

  6. Company accepts the credit

    The company logs in and accepts the ITC-02 on the portal; the credit then appears in its electronic credit ledger. Account for the matching inputs, stock and capital goods in the company's books.

  7. Cancel the old registration and update licences

    After the transfer, file for cancellation of the proprietorship's GSTIN (REG-16, reason: transfer of business), and move bank accounts, Udyam, IEC, shop licence and customer and vendor master data to the company.

The tax-neutral succession conditions

Under section 47(xiv) of the 1961 Act, the transfer of a capital asset by a sole proprietary concern to a company on succession is not treated as a transfer for capital gains, if three conditions are met. If any of them is later breached, section 47A(3) withdraws the exemption and the gain is taxed in the company's hands in the year of the breach.

Because the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, check how these conditions are carried into the new Act before the BTA is finalised, and have the valuation and consideration structured accordingly.

  • All assets and liabilities of the proprietorship relating to the business immediately before the succession become assets and liabilities of the company.
  • The proprietor's shareholding in the company is not less than 50% of the total voting power and continues to remain so for five years from the date of succession.
  • The proprietor receives no consideration or benefit, directly or indirectly, other than by way of allotment of shares in the company.

Practical points on the credit ledger

Only the balance in the electronic credit ledger on the date of transfer is moved, and only when the transfer includes a specific provision for liabilities. Make sure the inputs, semi-finished goods and capital goods to which the credit relates actually move to the company and are recorded in its books — the transferee must account for them.

File ITC-02 before applying to cancel the proprietorship's registration. Once the old GSTIN is cancelled, the ledger is frozen and the transfer can no longer be made from the portal.

Common pitfalls

Cancelling the old GSTIN first

If the proprietorship's registration is cancelled before ITC-02 is filed and accepted, the credit can be stranded. Sequence: company GSTIN, BTA, ITC-02, acceptance, then cancellation.

BTA without a liabilities clause

Section 18(3) and rule 41 require a specific provision for the transfer of liabilities. A BTA that transfers only assets does not support the credit transfer.

Paying the proprietor in cash

Any consideration other than shares — including a cash balance paid out — can break the tax-neutral conditions.

Diluting below 50% too early

Bringing in investors in the first five years can push the proprietor below 50% of voting power and withdraw the exemption. Model fundraising before you convert.

Forgetting licences and contracts

Customer contracts, tenders, Udyam, IEC and FSSAI or trade licences are in the proprietor's name. Novate or re-apply, or invoices from the new GSTIN may be rejected.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the proprietorship's GSTIN for the new company?

No. GST registration is PAN-based and the company has its own PAN, so it must obtain its own GSTIN. The credit balance can be moved to the new GSTIN through Form ITC-02.

Who certifies Form ITC-02?

Rule 41 of the CGST Rules requires a certificate from a practising chartered accountant or cost accountant certifying that the business was transferred with a specific provision for the transfer of liabilities.

Is GST payable on transferring the business to the company?

The transfer of a business as a going concern — the whole business or an independent part of it — is notified as a service with a nil GST rate. Transferring only selected assets is not a going-concern transfer and can attract GST on those assets.

Is the conversion free of capital gains tax?

It can be, if the succession conditions (all assets and liabilities transferred, at least 50% voting power held for five years, consideration only in shares) are met. With the Income-tax Act, 2025 in force from 1 April 2026, confirm the current provision and conditions with a tax adviser first.
