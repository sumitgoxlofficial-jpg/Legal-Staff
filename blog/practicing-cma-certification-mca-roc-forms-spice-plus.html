Practicing CMA Certification for SPICe+, INC-20A & ROC Forms

A practising Cost Accountant can certify most MCA e-forms, including SPICe+ and INC-20A. Here is where CMAs stand alongside CAs and CSs, and what a proper certificate involves.

Executive summary

Many MCA e-forms must be certified by a professional in whole-time practice before they are filed. Under the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014, that professional can generally be a chartered accountant, a company secretary or a cost accountant in practice. For incorporation, section 7(1)(b) of the Companies Act, 2013 specifically lists an advocate, a chartered accountant, a cost accountant or a company secretary in practice as the professional who can make the declaration of compliance.

So a practising CMA can certify SPICe+, INC-20A, DIR-3 KYC, INC-22, DIR-12, PAS-3, CHG-1, AOC-4 and most event-based forms. A small number of documents are reserved by the Act for company secretaries in practice — the MGT-8 certification of the annual return for prescribed companies, the signature on MGT-7 for those companies, and the secretarial audit report (MR-3). Cost audit, by contrast, is reserved for cost accountants.

A good certificate is more than a DSC on a form. It rests on a check of original documents, a working file, a UDIN generated on the institute's portal and a professional who is registered as such on MCA V3. That is what protects both the company and the certifying member if the filing is questioned later.

Step-by-step: how a practising CMA certifies an MCA form

  1. Confirm the form allows CMA certification

    Check the certification block in the e-form. Most forms offer chartered accountant, company secretary or cost accountant in whole-time practice; a few documents are reserved for one profession by the Act.

  2. Verify originals and the board record

    Inspect the source documents — identity and address proofs for SPICe+, the bank statement showing subscription money for INC-20A, the board resolution and consent for DIR-12 — and match them to the form fields.

  3. Keep a working file

    Record what was checked, by whom and when. Keep copies of the documents relied on. This is your evidence if the ROC, the institute or a court asks how the certification was made.

  4. Generate the UDIN

    Generate a Unique Document Identification Number on the ICMAI UDIN portal for the certificate, as the Institute requires for certificates issued by its members, and quote it where the form or the certificate allows.

  5. Sign with your DSC on MCA V3

    The certifying member uses a valid Class 3 DSC associated with a professional user ID on MCA V3, with membership number and certificate-of-practice details that MCA can validate.

  6. File and retain the acknowledgement

    Once the company files, keep the SRN and challan in the working file. If remarks are raised, re-verify any changed facts before re-certifying.

Why companies use a CMA desk for certification

For founders the question is usually practical: who can sign today, verify properly, and stand behind the certificate? Practising cost accountants are part of the same certification framework as CAs and CSs for most MCA forms, and they also hold the exclusive role in cost audit and a recognised role in GST certificates (such as ITC-02) and FEMA valuations.

Using one desk for SPICe+, INC-20A, CAS-4 or CMA data reports, and the valuation for an FC-GPR means the same professional understands the company's capital, address and business — fewer inconsistencies between filings.

  • A certificate should be issued only after the professional has seen the evidence — not on the basis of a scanned form alone.
  • If the company has an in-house CS, a practising CMA can still certify forms the rules allow, but MGT-8 and secretarial audit stay with a CS in practice.
  • Professional fees for certification are separate from MCA government fees, which are paid at actuals.

What the certifying professional checks on the common forms

The checks differ by form, but the principle is the same: every material fact in the form should be traceable to a document the professional has seen. For SPICe+, that means identity and address proofs of every subscriber and director, the registered-office proofs and owner NOC, the consents and declarations, and the MoA and AoA as signed.

For INC-20A, the focus is on money: a bank statement of the company showing that each subscriber has paid the value of the shares agreed in the MoA, and — where applicable — the approval of any sectoral regulator. For DIR-3 KYC, the professional checks the director's identity, address, mobile number and email against original records, because the OTPs and DIN status depend on them.

For event-based forms such as DIR-12 (change in directors), PAS-3 (allotment) and SH-7 (change in capital), the checks centre on the board and shareholder resolutions, the notice and quorum, the consents, and whether the event was filed within 30 days. A late form is still certified — but the additional fee, and any penalty exposure, should be pointed out to the company in writing.

Common pitfalls

Certifying without seeing originals

A DSC on a form is a professional statement. Always verify the source documents and keep a record.

No UDIN on the certificate

Certificates without the institute's UDIN may be rejected by banks and authorities and can lead to disciplinary questions.

Expired DSC or unmapped professional ID

An expired DSC or a professional account without validated membership details blocks the upload on MCA V3.

Using a CMA where the Act reserves the role

MGT-8 and secretarial audit belong to CSs in practice; cost audit belongs to CMAs. Check before engaging.

Certifying an old INC-20A bank statement

INC-20A rests on proof that subscribers actually paid. A statement that does not show the subscription credits is not enough.

Frequently asked questions

Can a practising cost accountant certify SPICe+?

Yes. Section 7(1)(b) of the Companies Act, 2013 allows the declaration to be made by an advocate, a chartered accountant, a cost accountant or a company secretary in practice who is engaged in the formation, and the e-form certification can be done by a practising CA, CS or CMA.

Which forms can a CMA not certify?

Documents reserved by the Act for company secretaries in practice — such as the MGT-8 certificate on the annual return of prescribed companies and the secretarial audit report — cannot be certified by a CMA. Always check the certification block on the current form.

Is UDIN mandatory for CMA certificates?

The Institute of Cost Accountants of India requires its members to generate a UDIN for certificates they issue. Many banks and authorities also check UDIN before accepting a certificate.

How quickly can a certificate be issued?

If the documents are complete, many certifications can be done on the same day. Delays usually come from missing originals, mismatched details or an expired DSC — so send everything together.
