Registering a Pvt Ltd on a Residential or Virtual Office: NOC & Geo-Coordinates

A home or a virtual office can be your registered office, but only with the right proofs and a real ability to receive notices. Here is what MCA V3 and the ROC check.

Executive summary

Section 12 of the Companies Act, 2013 requires every company to have a registered office capable of receiving and acknowledging all communications and notices. The law does not require a commercial building: a director's home or a serviced or virtual office can be used, provided the company can prove its right to use the premises and the address genuinely works.

The proofs are the same in substance whether you give the address in SPICe+ at incorporation or later in INC-22: a recent utility bill for the premises, a no-objection certificate from the owner, and a rent or lease agreement where the premises are rented. MCA has also been tightening verification — the ROC has a specific power to physically verify a registered office, and the V3 forms capture more detail about the location, including geo-coordinates and photographs in the current INC-22.

Virtual offices are where most problems arise. If the provider cannot reliably receive and pass on statutory notices, or a physical verification finds no trace of the company, the consequences range from penalties to action for striking off. GST registration often scrutinises the same address separately.

Step-by-step: setting up a home or virtual registered office

  1. Confirm the right to use the premises

    For a home owned by a director or relative, get a signed NOC from the owner. For rented premises, keep a rent or leave-and-licence agreement in the company's or a director's name plus the owner's NOC. For a virtual office, ask the provider for an agreement, the owner's NOC and a utility bill.

  2. Get a recent utility bill

    Electricity, telephone, gas or water bill for the exact address, generally not older than two months at the time of filing. The address on the bill should match the address you type into the form.

  3. Check the society or building rules

    Some housing societies and leases restrict commercial use. A registered office used only for correspondence is usually tolerated, but check — a dispute with the owner later can force a change of registered office.

  4. Capture the location details

    The current MCA V3 forms ask for the full address with PIN, and the current INC-22 also asks for latitude/longitude and photographs of the office. Take clear, geo-tagged photos showing the building and the company's name board.

  5. File the address with SPICe+ or INC-22

    If the address is ready at incorporation, give it in SPICe+ Part B with the proofs. Otherwise file INC-22 within 30 days of incorporation. Any later change also needs INC-22 (and other approvals if the change is to another state or ROC jurisdiction).

  6. Put up the name board

    Section 12(3) requires the company's name and registered-office address to be painted or affixed outside every office. A visible board is also your best evidence at a physical verification.

  7. Set up a notice-handling routine

    Agree in writing how the virtual-office provider will receive, scan and forward post and notices — and how fast. Missing a notice from the ROC or a tax authority because of the address is your company's problem, not the provider's.

Home office vs virtual office vs coworking: what the ROC sees

A residential address used by a founder is the simplest case: the owner's NOC and the utility bill come from people you know, and someone is at the address to receive post. Its weakness is privacy — the address becomes public on the MCA master data — and society rules.

A coworking seat with a dedicated desk usually sits in between. You have a genuine right to occupy, a name board is easy, and staff receive post. A pure virtual office, where nobody from the company ever works there, is the weakest at a physical verification and is often questioned by GST officers too. If you use one, keep the agreement current, ensure your name is displayed and test the notice-forwarding process.

  • GST registration has its own place-of-business proof and verification. An address acceptable to the ROC may still draw questions from a GST officer.
  • Moving the registered office within the same city needs a board resolution and INC-22; moving across ROC or state jurisdictions needs further approvals and alteration of the MoA.
  • Keep copies of the NOC, agreement and photos in the statutory records so you can answer a verification quickly.

What happens at a physical verification

Section 12(9) lets the Registrar physically verify a company's registered office if there is reason to believe the company is not carrying on business or operations there. Rule 25B of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 sets out the procedure: the verification is done in the presence of independent witnesses from the locality, and the officer records the visit with photographs of the premises.

If the registered office is found not capable of receiving and acknowledging communications, the Registrar can issue notice and, if the default is not explained, start action to remove the company's name from the register under section 248. Answering quickly with the NOC, agreement, utility bill, photos of the name board and evidence that notices are being received is usually the way out — which is why those documents belong in the statutory file, not in a founder's email.

Common pitfalls

NOC signed by someone who does not own the premises

The NOC should come from the owner named in the utility bill or property papers. A tenant's NOC for sub-letting without the owner's consent is weak proof.

Address typed differently from the bill

Flat numbers, floor and PIN must match. Small differences produce resubmission remarks.

Virtual office with no name board

If a physical verification finds no sign of the company, the ROC can start action. Insist that the provider displays your name.

Letting the virtual-office agreement lapse

Many providers' agreements run for 11 months. If it lapses, the company may no longer have a valid registered office.

Missing statutory notices

Show-cause notices sent to the registered office are treated as served. Agree written turnaround times for scanning and forwarding mail.

Frequently asked questions

Can I register my private limited company at my home address?

Yes. A residential address can be the registered office if you can provide a recent utility bill, the owner's NOC and, if rented, the rent agreement — and the address can actually receive notices. Check that your housing society or lease does not prohibit it.

Is a virtual office legal as a registered office?

The Companies Act does not prohibit it. The address must be capable of receiving and acknowledging communications, and you need valid proofs of the right to use it. The risk lies in physical verification and GST checks, so choose a provider that displays your name and forwards mail reliably.

How old can the utility bill be?

The MCA practice is that the utility bill should generally not be older than two months at the time of filing. If your filing is delayed or resubmitted, obtain a fresh bill.

What are the geo-coordinates asked for in INC-22?

The current INC-22 web form on MCA V3 captures the latitude and longitude of the registered office and photographs of the premises. Take the photographs on site with location enabled, showing the building and the name board. Field requirements change between form versions, so check the live form before filing.
