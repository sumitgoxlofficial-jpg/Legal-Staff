POSH Compliance & ICC External Member Staffing for 10+ Employee Startups

Once you have ten employees, the POSH Act requires an Internal Committee with an external member. Here is how to constitute it, run it and report on it without treating it as a formality.

Executive summary

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013, commonly called the POSH Act, requires every employer with ten or more employees to constitute an Internal Committee (often still called the ICC, or Internal Complaints Committee) at each office or administrative unit. "Employee" is defined broadly and includes regular, temporary, ad hoc and daily-wage workers, consultants, trainees, interns and those engaged through contractors.

The Committee must be presided over by a woman employed at a senior level, include at least two employee members, and include one external member from a non-governmental organisation or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with the issues relating to sexual harassment. At least half of the members must be women. Startups often find the external member hardest to source.

This guide explains how to constitute the Committee, what the employer must do every year, how the annual report works and what the penalties are. It is a general guide; for any live complaint, take advice from a qualified professional promptly.

Setting up POSH compliance, step by step

  1. Count employees the way the Act does

    Include everyone within the Act's definition of employee: full-time, part-time, temporary, interns, trainees, consultants and contract workers placed at your workplace. If the count is ten or more at a workplace, you need an Internal Committee there. Workplaces with fewer than ten employees fall under the Local Committee constituted by the District Officer.

  2. Appoint the Presiding Officer and employee members

    The Presiding Officer must be a woman employed at a senior level. If none is available at that office, the Act allows nomination from other offices or units of the same employer. Add at least two employee members, preferably committed to the cause of women or with experience in social work or legal knowledge.

  3. Engage the external member

    Appoint one member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment, meeting the criteria in the POSH Rules. The external member is paid fees or allowances for holding proceedings, as the employer prescribes. Check for conflicts of interest and record the appointment in writing.

  4. Issue the constitution order and policy

    Issue a written order constituting the Committee, with names and contact details and a term of not more than three years. Adopt a POSH policy that explains what sexual harassment is, how to complain, the inquiry process, confidentiality and the consequences.

  5. Display, train and orient

    Section 19 requires the employer to display the penal consequences of sexual harassment and the order constituting the Committee at a conspicuous place, organise workshops and awareness programmes at regular intervals, and run orientation for Committee members.

  6. Run inquiries within the statutory time frames

    A complaint is generally to be made within three months of the incident (extendable by up to three months for sufficient reasons). The inquiry must be completed within 90 days, and the report given within 10 days of completion. The employer acts on the recommendations within 60 days. Conciliation may be tried at the complainant's request, but no monetary settlement can be the basis of conciliation.

  7. File the annual report and disclose

    The Committee prepares an annual report and submits it to the employer and the District Officer. The employer also includes the number of cases filed and their disposal in its own annual report, and companies include the POSH statements required in the Board's report. Some states run online portals for registration or annual returns; check the requirement in yours.

Staffing the external member properly

The external member is there to bring independence and expertise. That means the person should not be an employee, a client or a vendor relationship that could compromise neutrality. Founders sometimes appoint a friend or a family member; that defeats the purpose and invites challenge to any inquiry.

Look for someone who meets the criteria in the POSH Rules, typically a person associated with an NGO working on women's rights, a person with social work experience in women's empowerment, or a person familiar with labour, service, civil or criminal law. Agree the fee per sitting or per inquiry in writing, set expectations on availability and turnaround, and include confidentiality obligations.

  • Written appointment letter with term, fee and confidentiality
  • Conflict-of-interest declaration
  • Orientation on your policy and the Committee's procedure
  • Back-up arrangement if the member is unavailable or conflicted

Confidentiality and record-keeping

Section 16 prohibits publishing or making known the contents of the complaint, the identities of the parties and witnesses, the inquiry proceedings and the recommendations, with limited exceptions. Section 17 provides for penalties on persons who breach this. Keep Committee records separately from general HR files, restrict access, and record attendance and decisions carefully.

Common pitfalls

Counting only full-time employees

The definition includes interns, consultants, contract staff and others. Many startups cross ten earlier than they think.

One Committee for many offices

Section 4 requires a Committee at each office or administrative unit, which matters for multi-city teams.

An external member with a conflict

A friend, investor or vendor in the role weakens every inquiry. Appoint someone genuinely independent.

Missing the annual report

The Committee's annual report is required even if there were no complaints. File a nil report.

Treating the policy as paperwork

Training, display of information and a working complaint channel are statutory duties under section 19.

Frequently asked questions

Does a startup with 10 employees need a POSH committee?

Yes. An employer with 10 or more employees at a workplace must constitute an Internal Committee there. The count includes temporary staff, interns, trainees, consultants and contract workers within the Act's definition of employee.

Who can be the external member of the Internal Committee?

A person from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment, who meets the criteria in the POSH Rules. They should be independent of the company and paid fees or allowances as the employer prescribes.

What is the penalty for not having an Internal Committee?

Section 26 provides a fine of up to ₹50,000. A repeat contravention can attract twice the punishment and cancellation or non-renewal of the licence or registration needed to carry on the business.

Is a POSH annual report required if there are no complaints?

Yes. The Committee's annual report goes to the employer and the District Officer every year, and a nil report should be filed where there were no complaints. Companies also include POSH disclosures in the Board's report.
