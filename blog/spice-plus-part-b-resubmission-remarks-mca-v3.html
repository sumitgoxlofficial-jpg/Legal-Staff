Fixing SPICe+ Part B Resubmission Remarks on MCA V3

The ROC has sent your SPICe+ back with remarks. Here is how to read them, fix every point in one pass and resubmit on the V3 portal without losing your name.

Executive summary

When the Central Registration Centre (CRC) or the Registrar examines a SPICe+ (INC-32) application and finds something it cannot approve as filed, it marks the application for resubmission and records remarks against the SRN. The application is not rejected yet: you get a limited window to correct the defects and send it back. If the defects are not cured — or the reply is incomplete — the next outcome is usually rejection, and a rejected SPICe+ can also cost you the reserved name.

Most remarks fall into a handful of buckets: objects in the e-MoA (INC-33) that do not match the name or the NIC activity code, a registered-office proof that is too old or not in the owner's name, signature or identity mismatches between the PAN, Aadhaar and the form, incomplete subscriber or director details, and AGILE-PRO-S (INC-35) or linked-form errors. Each is fixable, but only if you answer the remark that was actually raised and check every linked form for knock-on changes.

The practical rule is to treat a resubmission as a fresh filing: read all remarks, decide which forms and attachments change, re-sign with every DSC that the change touches, get the professional's declaration refreshed, and resubmit well before the deadline shown on the portal — not on the last day.

Step-by-step: resubmitting SPICe+ Part B on MCA V3

  1. Pull the exact remarks from the portal

    Log in to the MCA V3 account used to file the application and open the application history for the SRN. Download the remarks and the notice email. Note the resubmission due date shown there — that date, not a rule of thumb, is the one that governs your case.

  2. Map each remark to the form it affects

    List every remark and mark whether it changes SPICe+ Part B itself, the e-MoA (INC-33), the e-AoA (INC-34), AGILE-PRO-S (INC-35), INC-9 declarations or an attachment. A change to the objects clause, for example, often needs consistent edits in the MoA, the NIC code in Part B and sometimes the proposed name rationale.

  3. Fix the documents, not just the form fields

    If the remark is about the registered-office proof, obtain a fresh utility bill (generally not older than two months) and a properly signed NOC. If it is about identity, correct the spelling or date of birth at the source (PAN or Aadhaar) or align the form with the source. Re-scan everything legibly in colour.

  4. Update the linked web forms

    Open the linked forms from the resubmission screen and make the consequential changes. Check that names, addresses, shareholding and capital figures are identical across Part B, the MoA subscription page and AGILE-PRO-S. Inconsistency between linked forms is a common reason for a second round of remarks.

  5. Re-affix DSCs and refresh the professional declaration

    Where the content of a signed form changes, the subscribers, directors and the certifying professional normally need to sign again. Confirm every DSC is valid, Class 3 and mapped to the right user. The practising CA, CS or CMA should re-verify the changed documents before re-certifying.

  6. Write a short covering reply

    Where the portal allows a response or an optional attachment, give a point-wise reply: remark, what was changed, where to find it. Examiners process many applications; a clear reply reduces the chance of the same point being raised again.

  7. Resubmit early and track the SRN

    Submit, save the acknowledgement and watch the SRN status and email. If a further remark is raised, respond within the new window. Once approved, download the Certificate of Incorporation, PAN and TAN, and diarise INC-20A (180 days) and the auditor appointment (30 days).

Reading the most common remarks — and what they really mean

Remarks are short and often written in shorthand. Behind most of them is one of a few concerns: the examiner cannot verify a fact from your attachments, two parts of the application disagree, or the objects and name do not fit together.

Treat the remark as the minimum fix. If the examiner says the utility bill is outdated, also check that the NOC is signed by the person named on the bill and that the address in Part B matches the bill character for character. Fixing only the literal remark is the most common reason for a second resubmission.

  • "Objects not in line with name" — the activity word in the name (for example "Fintech" or "Foods") should be supported by the main objects in the MoA.
  • "Address proof not acceptable" — bill too old, not in the owner's name, partial address, or the NOC missing or unsigned.
  • "Attachment not legible / incomplete" — scans cut off, pages missing, or a password-protected PDF.
  • "Particulars of subscriber/director mismatch" — name, father's name or date of birth differs from PAN or the identity proof.
  • "Clarify/attach declaration" — a declaration or consent is missing, or signed by the wrong person.

When to stop resubmitting and refile instead

Occasionally the honest answer is that the application cannot be cured — for example, the proposed name is objected to on grounds you cannot meet, or the registered office is no longer available. In those cases it can be faster to let the application lapse and file afresh with a new name or address than to keep answering remarks.

Before you decide, check how many resubmissions remain and how long the name is still reserved. A practising professional can tell you whether the remark is one that is usually accepted on clarification or one that signals a deeper problem.

Common pitfalls

Answering only the literal remark

Examiners re-check the whole application. Fix the remark and everything connected to it, or you will see a fresh set of remarks.

Editing one form and forgetting the linked forms

A change in capital, shareholding or objects must flow into the MoA, AoA, Part B and AGILE-PRO-S. Mismatches between them are an easy objection.

Using an expired or wrong DSC

A DSC that expired between the first filing and the resubmission, or one registered to a different user, blocks the upload. Check validity before you start.

Waiting for the last day

Portal slowdowns around deadlines are common. Missing the resubmission window usually means the application is rejected and you start again.

Re-using an old utility bill

If weeks have passed since the original filing, the bill you used may now be too old. Get a fresh one dated within the last two months.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I get to resubmit SPICe+ after a remark?

The window is shown against your SRN on the MCA V3 portal and in the notice email; it is commonly 15 days. Treat the date on the portal as final and resubmit a few days before it, because the application can be rejected if the window passes.

Do I pay the MCA fee again when I resubmit SPICe+?

No new filing fee is charged for a resubmission of the same SRN. You may incur fresh costs if documents change — for example additional stamp duty if authorised capital is increased, or a new DSC if one has expired.

Will I lose my approved company name if SPICe+ Part B is rejected?

The name reserved through Part A is valid only for a limited period (20 days for a new company, unless extended). If Part B is rejected and the reservation lapses, the name becomes available to others, so a fresh application may need a fresh name approval.

Does every director need to sign again after resubmission?

Anyone whose signed form or declaration changes should re-sign with a valid DSC. If the change only affects an attachment, fewer signatures may be needed, but the professional certifying the form must re-verify and re-sign. Check the signing blocks shown on the resubmission screen.
