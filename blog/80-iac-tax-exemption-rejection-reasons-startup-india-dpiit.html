Startup India DPIIT Recognition vs 80-IAC Tax Holiday: How to Pass the IMB Review

DPIIT recognition is the first gate; the 80-IAC tax holiday needs a separate certificate from the Inter-Ministerial Board. Here is what the Board looks for and why applications fail.

Executive summary

Two things are often confused. DPIIT recognition under the Startup India programme makes an entity a "startup" for government schemes and certain regulatory relaxations. The tax holiday under section 80-IAC of the Income-tax Act, 1961 is a separate benefit: a deduction of 100% of profits from an eligible business for any three consecutive years out of the first ten years from incorporation. To claim it, a recognised startup must also obtain a certificate of eligible business from the Inter-Ministerial Board of Certification (IMB).

Many recognised startups never apply for the IMB certificate, and applications that do go in are reviewed on substance. The review focuses on whether the business is genuinely engaged in innovation, development or improvement of products, processes or services, or has a scalable model with high potential for employment or wealth creation. A good application shows this with evidence, not adjectives.

Two updates to keep in mind. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026; the startup deduction is carried into the new law under a different section number, so check the corresponding provision and transition rules with your tax adviser. And the incorporation window for 80-IAC has been extended by Finance Acts over time; confirm the current cut-off date before applying.

From DPIIT recognition to an IMB certificate

  1. Check entity type and age

    DPIIT recognition is available to private limited companies, registered partnership firms and LLPs within the age and turnover limits in the DPIIT notification. The 80-IAC deduction, however, is only for companies and LLPs. A partnership firm can be recognised but cannot claim 80-IAC, so convert first if the tax holiday matters.

  2. Check the incorporation window and turnover

    For 80-IAC the entity must be incorporated on or after 1 April 2016 and before the cut-off date in the current law (extended over time; most recently to 1 April 2030 as we understand it — confirm). Turnover must not exceed ₹100 crore in the year for which the deduction is claimed. DPIIT recognition separately requires turnover not exceeding ₹100 crore in any financial year since incorporation.

  3. Get DPIIT recognition first

    Apply on the Startup India portal with the certificate of incorporation, a description of the innovation and the problem solved, and supporting material such as a pitch deck, website or product links, and any patents or awards. Recognition is a prerequisite for the IMB application.

  4. Decide which three years you will claim

    The deduction is for any three consecutive assessment years out of ten from incorporation. Early-stage startups often have losses, so choose a block where profits are expected. Remember that a company claiming 80-IAC remains subject to minimum alternate tax, and a company that opts for the concessional regime under section 115BAA of the 1961 Act gives up Chapter VI-A deductions such as 80-IAC. Model both before committing.

  5. Prepare Form 1 for the IMB

    The application for the 80-IAC certificate is made in Form 1 on the Startup India portal. Expect to provide the memorandum or LLP agreement, a board resolution, financial statements and income-tax returns for the years available, and a clear write-up of the innovation and its scalability, ideally with a short product video or demo, patents filed, and evidence of traction.

  6. Answer clarifications quickly and precisely

    The IMB may seek clarifications. Answer the question asked, with documents. Vague restatements of the pitch rarely help.

  7. Claim the deduction correctly in the return

    Once certified, claim the deduction in the return for the chosen year, keep the IMB certificate on file and ensure the accounts separately identify income from the eligible business. Your tax adviser should check the corresponding provision under the Income-tax Act, 2025 for years governed by the new law.

Common reasons IMB applications fail

The IMB does not publish a list of rejection reasons, so the points below are drawn from the eligibility criteria and the kinds of clarification requests applicants commonly receive. They are patterns to avoid, not an official list.

  • The description reads like a conventional service (IT services, consulting, trading, agency) with no explanation of what is new.
  • Innovation is claimed but not evidenced: no product demo, no technical write-up, no patent or design filing, no users.
  • Scalability is asserted without a revenue model, unit economics or a credible path to employment generation.
  • The entity is ineligible: incorporated before 1 April 2016, a partnership firm, formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business, or over the turnover limit.
  • Documents conflict: different business descriptions in the MoA, the DPIIT application, the website and the financial statements.
  • Incomplete uploads, missing board resolution, or financial statements and returns that do not match.

Writing an application that survives review

Lead with the problem and the specific technical or business-model innovation that solves it. Explain why existing products do not, in two or three sentences. Then give evidence: a working product, pilot customers, patents filed, research outputs or awards. Finally, show scalability with numbers you can stand behind, such as current users, revenue, hiring plans, rather than market-size slides alone.

Keep the description consistent everywhere. If your object clause says "software development services", your DPIIT application says "AI diagnostics platform" and your financial statements show consulting revenue, expect questions. Align them before you apply.

Common pitfalls

Assuming recognition equals tax exemption

DPIIT recognition alone does not give 80-IAC. You need the IMB certificate.

Choosing the wrong three years

Claiming in loss years wastes the holiday. Plan the block of years against your projections.

Opting for section 115BAA without modelling

The concessional regime excludes 80-IAC. Compare tax under both before making the choice.

Applying as a partnership firm

Partnership firms can be recognised but cannot claim 80-IAC. Convert to a company or LLP first.

Ignoring the new Act's transition

From 1 April 2026, the 2025 Act applies. Have your adviser map the claim to the corresponding provision.

Frequently asked questions

Is DPIIT recognition enough to claim 80-IAC?

No. DPIIT recognition is a prerequisite, but the deduction requires a separate certificate of eligible business from the Inter-Ministerial Board, applied for in Form 1 on the Startup India portal.

Can an LLP claim the 80-IAC tax holiday?

Yes. Section 80-IAC is available to eligible companies and LLPs. A registered partnership firm can get DPIIT recognition but is not eligible for 80-IAC.

How many years of tax exemption does 80-IAC give?

A deduction of 100% of profits from the eligible business for any three consecutive years out of the first ten years from incorporation, subject to the turnover limit of ₹100 crore in the claim year and the other conditions. MAT continues to apply to companies.

Does 80-IAC still apply under the Income-tax Act, 2025?

The new Act, in force from 1 April 2026, carries forward a startup deduction under a renumbered section. Check the corresponding provision, its conditions and the transition rules with a tax adviser before claiming for any year governed by the new law.
