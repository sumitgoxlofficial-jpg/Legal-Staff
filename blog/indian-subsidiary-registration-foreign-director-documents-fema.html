Incorporating an Indian Subsidiary with Foreign Directors: Apostille & FEMA Checklist

Foreign parent, foreign directors, Indian company: the documents to apostille, the resident-director rule, and the FEMA reports that follow incorporation.

Executive summary

A foreign company can set up a wholly owned or majority-owned private limited company in India through SPICe+, the same route Indian promoters use. The differences are in the paperwork and the follow-on reporting: documents signed or issued abroad must be notarised and apostilled (or consularised), at least one director must be resident in India, and the share issue to the foreign parent is foreign direct investment that must be reported under FEMA.

Before you start, check the FDI policy for your sector. Most sectors are under the automatic route, but some have caps or need government approval, and investment from an entity of a country sharing a land border with India requires government approval under Press Note 3 (2020). That check decides whether incorporation can proceed in days or needs months.

After incorporation, the subscription money arrives through banking channels, shares are issued within the permitted time, and Form FC-GPR is filed on the RBI's FIRMS portal within 30 days of allotment. Annual FEMA and Companies Act compliances then follow, including the FLA return.

Step-by-step: incorporating a foreign-owned subsidiary

  1. Check the FDI route and sector conditions

    Confirm whether the sector is under the automatic or government route, any equity cap, and whether Press Note 3 applies to any investor in the ownership chain. Record the analysis; the bank and your auditors will ask for it.

  2. Fix the shareholding and directors

    A private company needs at least two shareholders and two directors. A wholly owned subsidiary usually has the parent as the main shareholder and a nominee (an individual or a group entity) holding one or a few shares. At least one director must satisfy the resident-director test.

  3. Collect and legalise foreign documents

    For foreign individuals: passport and a recent address proof. For the foreign parent: certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents as needed, and a board resolution authorising the investment, the subscription and an authorised signatory. Notarise and apostille them (Hague Convention countries) or have them consularised at the Indian mission (other countries).

  4. Get DSCs and plan DIN allotment

    Foreign directors can obtain Class 3 DSCs from Indian certifying authorities using passport-based verification. DINs for up to three proposed directors can be allotted through SPICe+ itself.

  5. File SPICe+ with the parent as subscriber

    Reserve the name, then file Part B with the e-MoA and e-AoA signed for the parent by its authorised representative, the registered-office proofs and the professional's declaration. Open the bank account through AGILE-PRO-S or directly with an AD Category-I bank.

  6. Receive funds and allot shares

    The parent remits the subscription money from abroad through banking channels; the bank issues the inward remittance documents and completes KYC of the remitter. Shares must be issued within 60 days of receipt of the money, or it must be refunded, as provided in the FEMA non-debt instruments rules.

  7. Report on FIRMS and file post-incorporation forms

    File FC-GPR on the FIRMS portal within 30 days of allotment, with the valuation certificate and the declarations and certificates the form asks for. Then complete INC-20A within 180 days, appoint the first auditor within 30 days, and diarise the FLA return (due 15 July each year).

Apostille or consularisation: getting foreign documents accepted

India is a party to the Hague Apostille Convention. A document executed in another member country is usually first notarised there and then apostilled by that country's designated authority. For countries that are not party to the Convention, the document is notarised and then legalised (consularised) by the Indian embassy or consulate.

Plan this early: legalisation can take weeks. Make sure the name on the passport, the address proof and the DSC application match exactly, and that the parent's board resolution names the authorised signatory who will sign the MoA and AoA and handle the share subscription.

  • Address proofs for foreign individuals should be recent; a bank statement, utility bill or government-issued residence document is typical.
  • If a document is not in English, a certified translation is needed along with the original.
  • Keep the originals or certified copies on file — the bank and auditors will ask for them again.

The resident-director rule after incorporation

Section 149(3) requires every company to have at least one director who has stayed in India for a total of at least 182 days during the financial year. For a company incorporated part-way through a year, the requirement applies proportionately. Many foreign groups appoint an India-based employee, a local business head, or an independent professional to meet it.

A resident director is not just a box to tick. They sign filings, receive notices and carry officer-in-default exposure, so choose someone who will actually be involved and give them proper indemnity and D&O cover.

Common pitfalls

Ignoring Press Note 3 in the ownership chain

The test looks at beneficial ownership, not just the immediate parent. Map the full chain before incorporation.

Sending the money before the bank is ready

Remittances to an account that is not yet fully set up, or without the purpose code and remitter details, create reporting delays. Coordinate with the AD bank first.

Missing the 60-day allotment window

If shares are not allotted within 60 days of receipt, the money must be refunded, or the delay regularised. Diarise it from the date of credit.

No valuation certificate at allotment

Even at incorporation-stage pricing, keep a fair-value certificate from a CA, merchant banker or practising cost accountant to file with FC-GPR.

Choosing a resident director in name only

A director who is not engaged cannot sign on time or answer notices, and carries real liability. Pick someone involved in the business.

Frequently asked questions

Can all directors of an Indian subsidiary be foreign nationals?

A company may have foreign directors, but at least one director must be resident in India — having stayed in India for at least 182 days in the financial year (proportionately in the first year). The others can be foreign nationals.

Is FC-GPR needed for subscriber shares issued at incorporation?

Yes. Shares issued to a person resident outside India — including on subscription to the MoA — are reported in FC-GPR on the FIRMS portal within 30 days of allotment. Your AD bank processes the form.

Do foreign documents need apostille or consular attestation?

Documents executed abroad should be notarised and apostilled if the country is a party to the Hague Apostille Convention, or notarised and consularised by the Indian mission if it is not. Non-English documents also need a certified translation.

How long does it take to set up an Indian subsidiary?

The MCA part can be quick once documents are ready, but the overall timeline depends on legalising foreign documents, DSCs, bank KYC for the parent and whether any government approval is needed. Most of the time is in preparation, not in the SPICe+ filing.
