CAS-4 Certification Explained: Captive Consumption & Job-Work Valuation Under GST

Supplying goods to your own branch in another state, or to a related company, can require a cost-based value under GST. CAS-4 is how that cost is measured and certified.

Executive summary

Under GST, a supply between distinct persons (for example two GST registrations of the same company in different states) or between related persons is valued under rule 28 of the CGST Rules: at open market value, or the value of goods of like kind and quality, and failing those, under rule 30 or rule 31. Rule 30 values the supply at 110% of the cost of production or manufacture, or the cost of acquisition, of the goods.

CAS-4 — the Cost Accounting Standard on cost of production for captive consumption issued by the Institute of Cost Accountants of India — is the established method for working out that cost. It sets out which costs go in (materials, direct employee cost, direct expenses, works overheads, a share of quality control, R&D and production-related administration) and which stay out (selling and distribution, interest, profit). A practising cost accountant certifies the computation, which you keep as support for the value declared.

Rule 28 also has a valuable relief: where the recipient is eligible for full input tax credit, the value declared in the invoice is deemed to be the open market value. In many inter-unit transfers this makes the exercise simpler — but not where the recipient's credit is restricted, such as units with exempt supplies.

Step-by-step: getting a CAS-4 based value in place

  1. Identify supplies that need a cost-based value

    List stock transfers to other GSTINs of the same entity, supplies to related parties, and any supplies where there is no comparable open market price. Note whether the recipient can take full ITC.

  2. Check rule 28 before going to rule 30

    Try open market value and like-kind-and-quality first. If the recipient is eligible for full ITC, the invoice value can be deemed open market value under rule 28. Use rule 30 only when the earlier tests do not give a value.

  3. Build the CAS-4 cost sheet

    Compile product-wise cost of production for the period: materials (net of recoverable taxes), direct employee cost, direct expenses, works overheads, quality control, R&D and production administration, adjusted for opening and closing work-in-progress and credits for scrap.

  4. Apply the 110% rule

    Under rule 30, the taxable value is 110% of the cost of production. Apply it per unit to transfers in the period, and update the cost periodically — many companies use the previous year's audited cost with a true-up.

  5. Get it certified by a practising CMA

    A cost accountant in practice reviews the cost sheet against the books, issues the CAS-4 certificate with a UDIN, and notes the method and period. Keep it with your GST records.

  6. Reconcile and review annually

    Reconcile the cost used for invoicing with the audited cost after year-end. Where the cost was materially understated, consider differential tax payment to avoid disputes.

What goes into CAS-4 cost of production

CAS-4 builds cost from the bottom up. The aim is the cost of producing the goods at the point they are transferred — not the price you would charge an outside customer.

  • Included: materials consumed (net of taxes for which credit is available), direct employee cost, direct expenses, works overheads, quality control, research and development, and administrative overheads relating to production.
  • Adjusted: opening and closing work-in-progress, credits for recoveries such as scrap and by-products.
  • Excluded: selling and distribution costs, interest and finance charges, abnormal losses, and profit.
  • Packing: primary packing needed to make the goods marketable is typically included; secondary packing for transport usually is not.

Job work: where CAS-4 does and does not matter

Sending goods to a job worker for processing is not a supply if the conditions of section 143 are met: the goods are sent under a challan and come back within one year (inputs) or three years (capital goods). The job worker charges GST on its job-work service. In this case CAS-4 valuation is not needed for the movement itself.

If the goods are not returned within the time limit, they are treated as supplied on the day they were sent out, and you will need to value them — which is where cost-based valuation and CAS-4 can come back into the picture.

Common pitfalls

Using rule 30 when rule 28 relief applies

If the recipient branch is eligible for full ITC, the invoice value can be deemed open market value. Check before building a full cost sheet.

Including interest and selling costs

CAS-4 excludes interest, selling and distribution costs and profit. Including them overstates value; excluding production overheads understates it.

Stale cost data

A cost sheet from several years ago does not reflect current input prices. Update periodically and reconcile with audited numbers.

No certificate on file

Without a cost accountant's certificate and working papers, a department audit is harder to defend.

Ignoring job-work time limits

Inputs not returned within one year (capital goods three years) become deemed supplies. Track challans and file ITC-04 on time.

Frequently asked questions

Is a CAS-4 certificate mandatory under GST?

GST law does not name CAS-4, but rule 30 requires a value based on cost of production, and CAS-4 is the recognised standard for computing it. A certificate from a practising cost accountant is the usual way to support the value in an audit.

What is the 110% rule in GST valuation?

Under rule 30 of the CGST Rules, where value cannot be determined under the earlier rules, the value of supply is 110% of the cost of production or manufacture, or of the cost of acquisition, of the goods.

Do stock transfers to another state need CAS-4 valuation?

A transfer to a GST registration of the same entity in another state is a supply between distinct persons. If the receiving unit is eligible for full ITC, the invoice value is deemed open market value under rule 28. Otherwise a cost-based value under rule 30, often computed using CAS-4, may be needed.

Who can issue a CAS-4 certificate?

CAS-4 is a standard of the Institute of Cost Accountants of India, and certificates are issued by cost accountants in practice, with a UDIN.
