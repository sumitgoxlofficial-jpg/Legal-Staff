SPICe+ Part A vs Direct Part B: Avoiding Rule 8 Name Rejections

Most name rejections come from three rules. Learn what rules 8, 8A and 8B test for, and when it is worth reserving the name before filing Part B.

Executive summary

A company name in India is tested against section 4 of the Companies Act, 2013 and rules 8, 8A and 8B of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014. Rule 8 deals with names that are identical to, or too nearly resemble, an existing company or LLP name. Rule 8A lists names that are undesirable for other reasons. Rule 8B lists words and expressions that can be used only with prior approval of the Central Government or a regulator.

You can apply for the name on its own — through SPICe+ Part A or the RUN (Reserve Unique Name) web service — or fill Part A and Part B together in one go. Reserving first costs a little time but isolates the name risk. Filing everything together is faster when the name is distinctive, but a name objection then holds up (and may force rework of) the whole incorporation package.

The best defence is a distinctive, coined first word, an activity word that matches your objects, and a trademark search before you file. Two good options proposed together, with a short note on the meaning of a coined word, clear more often than a single generic name.

Step-by-step: choosing and reserving a name that clears

  1. Shortlist distinctive names

    Start with a coined or uncommon first word. Avoid names that are only a dictionary word plus the activity, such as "Smart Software Private Limited" — these are likely to resemble existing names.

  2. Run the MCA name check and a trademark search

    Use the company/LLP name search on the MCA portal and search the trade marks registry for identical or similar marks in the classes relevant to your business. A registered trademark owned by someone else is a ground for objection and for later rectification.

  3. Match the activity word to your objects

    If the name carries an activity word (Foods, Infra, Fintech, Capital), the main objects in the MoA must support it. Words suggesting regulated activity may require regulator approval or registration.

  4. Screen for rule 8B words

    Words such as Bank, Insurance, Stock Exchange, Mutual Fund, Board, Commission, Authority, National, Union or Central typically need prior approval or a no-objection from the relevant authority. Drop them unless you genuinely have that approval.

  5. Decide: reserve first or file together

    Reserve first through Part A or RUN when the name is close to existing names, uses a sensitive word, or the client cares strongly about the exact name. File Part A with Part B together when the name is clearly distinctive and speed matters.

  6. Explain coined words

    Use the comments field to explain the meaning or origin of a coined word, and whether it is the promoter's trademark. This context helps the examiner and reduces "meaning of the word" remarks.

  7. File Part B within the reservation period

    Once approved, a name for a new company is reserved for 20 days. Plan documents and DSCs so Part B can be filed well within that window.

What "too nearly resembles" means in practice

Rule 8 tells the examiner to ignore differences that do not make a name distinctive. Adding or removing plurals, spaces, punctuation, articles such as "the", the type of company ("Private Limited"), a different tense, or a phonetically similar spelling generally does not make a name different from one that already exists.

So "Bluefin Labs Private Limited" and "Blue Fin Lab Private Limited" are likely to be treated as the same name. A genuinely different first word, not a cosmetic variation, is what clears rule 8.

  • Different spelling, same sound (Kwik vs Quick): usually treated as resembling.
  • Adding a place name or "India" to an existing name: usually not enough on its own.
  • Using a group company's name: generally allowed with a board resolution or consent from that company, which should be attached.

Part A first, or straight to Part B?

SPICe+ lets you fill Part A (name) and Part B (incorporation) together. That saves one round of waiting when the name is safe. If the name is objected to, however, you deal with the name and the rest of the application at the same time, and remarks on the name can delay everything else.

RUN or a standalone Part A is the cautious option when the name matters commercially — a brand that is already trading, a name already printed on packaging, or a client with investors who expect the exact name. The trade-off is a few extra working days.

Common pitfalls

Relying on the MCA search alone

The name search shows existing company and LLP names, not trademarks. A clear MCA search does not protect you from a trademark owner who later applies for rectification under section 16.

Activity words without matching objects

A name like "Arka Fintech Private Limited" with general trading objects invites a remark. Align the objects clause with the activity in the name.

Using regulated words casually

Words such as Bank, Insurance, Finance or Capital can attract scrutiny or require approvals. Use them only when the business model and approvals support them.

Two near-identical options

Proposing "Zenvo Tech" and "Zenvo Technologies" gives you one real chance, not two. Make the second option genuinely different.

Letting the reservation lapse

Twenty days passes quickly when DSCs and address proofs are still being arranged. Prepare the Part B documents before reserving the name.

Frequently asked questions

How many names can I propose in SPICe+ Part A or RUN?

You can propose up to two names in one application, in order of preference, and you get one chance to resubmit if the examiner raises remarks. Propose two genuinely different names rather than variations of the same one.

How long is an approved company name reserved?

For a new company, an approved name is reserved for 20 days from approval. Extensions on payment of a fee have been permitted under the rules; check the current options on the MCA portal before relying on one.

Can I use a name that is my registered trademark?

Yes, and it usually helps. Attach the trademark registration certificate or an authorisation from the trademark owner. If someone else owns the mark, the name can be objected to and, even if approved, may later have to be changed under section 16.

Is filing Part A and Part B together riskier?

Only if the name is likely to draw remarks. If the name is distinctive and searched, filing together is efficient. If it is close to existing names or uses sensitive words, reserve it first so a name problem does not hold up the rest of the incorporation.
