In-House Company Secretary vs Virtual Compliance Officer: Startup Cost Breakdown

Most early-stage private companies are not required to hire a whole-time company secretary. Here is where the legal line sits, what each option really costs and how to decide.

Executive summary

Section 203 of the Companies Act, 2013 and rules 8 and 8A of the Companies (Appointment and Remuneration of Managerial Personnel) Rules, 2014 decide when a company must have a whole-time company secretary (CS). For most early-stage private companies, the threshold is a paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more. Below that, there is no legal requirement for a whole-time CS, although every company still has to keep its secretarial records and filings in order.

That leaves a cost decision. An in-house CS gives full-time availability and deep context, but brings salary, benefits, management time and a single point of failure. A virtual compliance officer, an external team on a monthly retainer, gives broad coverage across MCA, secretarial and contract work at a lower fixed cost, but needs good information flow from the company.

This guide sets out the legal triggers, the cost components on each side, and a simple test for when to switch. Salary figures are indicative ranges only; they vary widely by city, sector and experience, so benchmark against current offers in your market.

Deciding between in-house and virtual, step by step

  1. Check whether a whole-time CS is mandatory

    Under rule 8, every listed company and every other public company with paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more must appoint whole-time key managerial personnel, including a CS. Under rule 8A, any other company with paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more must have a whole-time CS. If you are below these thresholds, the decision is commercial, not legal.

  2. Project when you will cross ₹10 crore paid-up capital

    Paid-up capital rises with each priced round. Note that the test is paid-up share capital, not the amount raised; securities premium does not count towards it. Model your cap table to see when you would cross the line, and plan the hire around six months before.

  3. List the work, not the job title

    Write down what needs doing each month: board and general meetings, minutes, registers, MCA filings (AOC-4, MGT-7 or MGT-7A, DIR-3 KYC, DPT-3, MSME-1 where applicable), share allotments, ESOP administration, FEMA reporting, contract reviews. This list is what you are buying, whichever option you choose.

  4. Cost the in-house option fully

    Add salary, statutory contributions, benefits, recruitment cost, laptop and software, training, ICSI membership and leave cover. Factor in the time a founder or CFO spends managing the role, and the risk of a gap if the person leaves.

  5. Cost the virtual option fully

    Ask for a fixed monthly retainer that names the deliverables and response times, plus how out-of-scope work (a funding round, a conversion, a notice reply) is priced. Government fees and stamp duty are always extra, at actuals.

  6. Check certification needs

    Certain filings need certification by a practising professional, such as MGT-8 for the annual return of larger companies or certification of e-forms. An employed CS cannot certify as a practising CS. Budget for an external practising professional either way.

  7. Review the choice every year

    Revisit after each funding round or when headcount, contract volume or regulatory exposure changes materially. Many companies move from virtual to a hybrid model, with an in-house CS supported by an external desk, before they become mandatory.

Indicative cost components

There is no reliable official salary survey for company secretaries in startups, so treat any figure as a starting point for negotiation. Broadly, a newly qualified CS in a metro may expect a fixed salary in the low-to-mid single-digit lakhs per year, and an experienced CS with five or more years in a funded company several times that, with significant variation by city and sector. Add employer contributions and benefits on top.

A virtual compliance officer is usually priced as a monthly retainer tied to a defined scope. The right comparison is not salary versus retainer, but total cost of the work list versus total cost of each option, including the founder time saved or spent.

  • In-house: salary, benefits, recruitment, tools, training, leave cover, management time.
  • Virtual: retainer, out-of-scope work, onboarding time, information-sharing discipline.
  • Both: practising professional certification, government fees and stamp duty at actuals.

A simple switching test

Move to an in-house CS when one or more of these is true: the law requires it; you hold board or shareholder meetings more than quarterly; you run continuous ESOP grants and allotments; you have regulated activity (for example FEMA-heavy foreign investment or an RBI or SEBI touchpoint) that needs daily attention; or your external retainer is consistently exceeding its scope. Until then, a virtual compliance officer usually covers the work at lower fixed cost.

Common pitfalls

Counting securities premium as paid-up capital

The ₹10 crore test is on paid-up share capital. Premium does not count. Check your actual figure before hiring or delaying.

Expecting an employee to certify forms

An in-house CS cannot sign certifications reserved for practising professionals. Budget for external certification.

Retainers without a scope

A vague retainer leads to disputes about what is included. Insist on a written deliverables list.

Leaving a KMP vacancy open

If you are required to have a CS and the role falls vacant, section 203(4) gives six months to fill it. Start recruiting immediately.

Frequently asked questions

Is a company secretary mandatory for a private limited company?

A whole-time CS is mandatory under rule 8A for a private company with paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more. Below that, there is no requirement, though the company must still comply with secretarial and filing obligations.

Can a virtual compliance officer replace a mandatory whole-time CS?

No. Where the law requires a whole-time CS, the company must appoint one as KMP. A virtual compliance team can support that CS but cannot replace the appointment.

What is the penalty for not appointing a CS when required?

Section 203(5) provides a penalty of ₹5 lakh on the company and ₹50,000 on each director and KMP in default, with ₹1,000 per day for continuing default up to ₹5 lakh. Confirm the current figures with your adviser.

How much does a company secretary cost in India?

It varies widely by city, sector and experience. Compare the full cost of employment, including benefits and management time, with a scoped retainer for the same work list rather than headline salary alone.
