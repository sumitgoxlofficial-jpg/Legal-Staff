Startup Share Valuation Under Income Tax & FEMA: Registered Valuer vs CMA/CA

One funding round can need three valuations for three laws. Here is who is allowed to sign each, and what changed after angel tax was abolished.

Executive summary

A priced funding round in an Indian private company can trigger valuation requirements under three separate laws, and each law names its own valuer. Under FEMA, shares issued to a non-resident must be priced at or above fair value, certified by a chartered accountant, a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practising cost accountant. Under the Companies Act, 2013, a preferential allotment by a private company under section 62(1)(c) needs a valuation report from a registered valuer under section 247. Income-tax rules have their own valuation methods and, for some purposes, specify a merchant banker.

The biggest recent change is on the income-tax side. The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 abolished the "angel tax" in section 56(2)(viib) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 for all classes of investors from assessment year 2025-26, so premium received on issuing shares is no longer taxed under that provision. Valuation under rule 11UA still matters for other purposes — for example where an investor or other recipient acquires shares below fair market value. The Income-tax Act, 2025 has applied from 1 April 2026 with renumbered sections and rules; confirm the current position and references with your tax adviser for each transaction.

In practice, one professional can often cover more than one role: a cost accountant or chartered accountant who is also an IBBI-registered valuer for securities or financial assets can sign both the FEMA certificate and the Companies Act report, if independence rules allow. The work is to map each requirement first, then decide who signs what.

Step-by-step: valuing shares for a funding round

  1. Map every law that applies

    Is any investor a non-resident (FEMA)? Is the issue a preferential allotment under section 62(1)(c) (Companies Act)? Are any shares being transferred, or issued to employees, where income-tax valuation applies? List each requirement before appointing a valuer.

  2. Choose the valuer for each requirement

    FEMA: CA, SEBI-registered merchant banker or practising cost accountant. Companies Act section 62(1)(c) and other section 247 cases: an IBBI-registered valuer. Income-tax: as specified in the current rules for the method used.

  3. Agree the valuation date and method

    Use a date close to the board approval of the issue. Discounted cash flow is common for early-stage companies; net asset value and market multiples are used where relevant. Under FEMA the method must be an internationally accepted pricing methodology on an arm's length basis.

  4. Prepare defensible projections

    Projections drive DCF value. Base them on the business plan the investors saw, with explained assumptions for growth, margins and capital needs. Keep the version history.

  5. Issue at or above the FEMA floor

    For shares issued to a non-resident, the issue price must not be less than the fair value in the certificate. Pass the board and shareholder resolutions, and complete the allotment within the FEMA timeline after receiving funds.

  6. File and keep the reports

    Attach the valuation certificate to FC-GPR on the FIRMS portal within 30 days of allotment, file PAS-3 for the allotment and MGT-14 for the special resolution where required, and keep all reports in the statutory records for audit and due diligence.

Registered valuer vs CMA or CA: not either-or

A "registered valuer" is a person registered with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, for a specific asset class such as securities or financial assets. Many registered valuers are cost accountants or chartered accountants who have passed the IBBI examination and joined a registered valuers organisation.

So the practical question is not "registered valuer or CMA?" but "which certificate does this law ask for, and does my professional hold that status?" A practising cost accountant can issue the FEMA pricing certificate in their own right. The same person can sign the section 62 report only if they are also a registered valuer for securities or financial assets.

  • Ask for the IBBI registration number and asset class if you need a Companies Act valuation.
  • Ask for the certificate of practice and UDIN for a FEMA certificate from a CA or CMA.
  • Check independence: the statutory auditor or an interested party may be restricted from acting.

After angel tax: why valuation still matters

The abolition of section 56(2)(viib) removed the tax on share premium, which was the main income-tax concern for startups raising money. Valuation is still needed for FEMA pricing, Companies Act allotments, secondary transfers, ESOP perquisites and, increasingly, for investor due diligence.

Because the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, provisions and rule numbers referred to in older valuation reports and shareholder agreements may now map to new sections. Ask your tax adviser to confirm the current provision before relying on a rule number in a new report.

Common pitfalls

One valuation for every purpose

A FEMA certificate from a CA does not satisfy section 62(1)(c) unless the signer is also a registered valuer. Map requirements first.

Stale valuation date

A report dated months before the allotment may not reflect fair value at issue. Keep the valuation date close to the board approval.

Issuing to non-residents below fair value

FEMA sets a floor for issues to non-residents. Pricing below it is a contravention that needs compounding.

Projections that differ from the pitch deck

Investors and authorities compare documents. Use one consistent set of projections, with documented changes.

Relying on old section numbers

With the Income-tax Act, 2025 in force, confirm the current provision before citing a 1961-Act section in a new report or agreement.

Frequently asked questions

Can a cost accountant value shares for FEMA?

Yes. Under the FEMA (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, the fair value of unlisted shares issued to a non-resident can be certified by a chartered accountant, a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practising cost accountant, using an internationally accepted pricing methodology on an arm's length basis.

Do I need a registered valuer for a startup funding round?

If the shares are issued by way of preferential allotment under section 62(1)(c) of the Companies Act, 2013, the price must be supported by a registered valuer's report. Other requirements — FEMA pricing, ESOP perquisites — have their own valuer rules.

Is angel tax still applicable to startups?

Section 56(2)(viib) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 was abolished for all classes of investors from assessment year 2025-26. The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, so confirm the current position with a tax adviser for your transaction, as other valuation-based provisions continue.

Which valuation method is used for early-stage startups?

Discounted cash flow is the most common for companies with limited history, sometimes cross-checked with recent transaction prices or market multiples. The method must be one the relevant law accepts and must be applied by the valuer that law names.
