Pre-Seed & Seed Legal Due Diligence Checklist: Cleaning Up MCA & Secretarial Records

Investor diligence will test your cap table against your MCA filings line by line. Clean up allotments, approvals, ESOPs, IP and FEMA filings before the term sheet, not after.

Executive summary

At pre-seed and seed stage, legal due diligence is less about complex risk and more about whether your records are complete and consistent. Investors' counsel will compare your cap table with the MCA record (PAS-3 returns of allotment, SH-7 for authorised capital, MGT-14 for special resolutions), your share certificates and register of members, and your board and shareholder approvals. Every mismatch becomes a condition precedent or a delay.

Beyond the cap table, diligence covers who owns the IP, whether founders and employees have signed the right agreements, whether an ESOP scheme was properly approved, and whether foreign investment was reported under FEMA. Fixing these before you sign a term sheet keeps the round on schedule and the price where you negotiated it.

This checklist follows the order most investor counsel use. Some fixes are simple filings; others, such as a missed private placement step or a late FEMA report, may need compounding or late submission fees and should be handled with professional advice.

A pre-diligence clean-up in seven steps

  1. Rebuild the cap table from source documents

    Start from the MoA subscribers and every allotment since. For each, collect the board resolution, shareholder resolution where needed, offer letter (PAS-4 for private placements), bank receipt, PAS-3 filing and share certificate. Reconcile the totals with the register of members and the shareholding in the last MGT-7 or MGT-7A.

  2. Check authorised capital and SH-7

    Every increase in authorised capital needs an ordinary resolution (or as the articles require) and Form SH-7 within 30 days under section 64. If allotments ever exceeded authorised capital on the date of issue, that needs specific advice.

  3. Verify each allotment route

    Private placements under section 42 need a special resolution, an offer letter in PAS-4, a separate bank account for application money, allotment within 60 days, and PAS-3 within 15 days of allotment. Rights issues follow section 62(1)(a). Preferential allotments under section 62(1)(c) need a valuation report from a registered valuer. Check each one against what was actually done.

  4. Review the ESOP scheme

    An ESOP needs a scheme approved by special resolution under section 62(1)(b) and rule 12 of the Share Capital and Debentures Rules, with a minimum vesting period of one year. Directors or promoters holding more than 10% are normally excluded, though startups have a relaxation for a period from incorporation. Keep the register of ESOPs in Form SH-6 and grant letters for every option holder.

  5. Confirm IP ownership

    Founders often build the product before incorporation. Take written assignments of copyright, code, designs and domain names from each founder and early contributor to the company. The Copyright Act, 1957 requires an assignment in writing; if duration or territory is not specified, statutory defaults apply that may not suit you. File trademark applications in the company's name.

  6. Check founder and employment agreements

    Investors look for founder agreements or articles provisions on vesting, roles, transfer restrictions and exits, and for employment and consultant agreements with confidentiality and IP assignment clauses. Non-compete clauses after employment are generally unenforceable in India under section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, so rely on confidentiality and non-solicit provisions instead.

  7. Verify FEMA filings for any foreign investment

    If any shareholder is a person resident outside India, check the pricing (not less than fair value under an internationally accepted method, certified as the rules require), Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment on the FIRMS portal, FC-TRS for transfers, and the annual return on foreign liabilities and assets (FLA) by 15 July. Late filings can usually be regularised with a late submission fee; other contraventions may need compounding.

The documents investor counsel will ask for

Have these in a well-indexed data room before the first diligence call. Missing items are fine to disclose; surprises are not.

  • Certificate of incorporation, current MoA and AoA, and every amendment with MGT-14
  • Register of members, share certificates (or demat records) and stamp duty proof
  • Board and shareholder minutes, attendance, and resolutions for every allotment
  • PAS-4, PAS-5 records of private placement, PAS-3 and SH-7 filings with receipts
  • Valuation reports for priced rounds and preferential allotments
  • ESOP scheme, special resolution, SH-6 register and grant letters
  • Founder agreements and IP assignment deeds; employment and consultant agreements
  • FEMA filings: FC-GPR, FC-TRS, FLA acknowledgements, and pricing certificates
  • Annual filings (AOC-4, MGT-7 or MGT-7A), DIR-3 KYC status and INC-20A

Significant beneficial ownership and nominee holdings

Under section 90 and the Significant Beneficial Owners Rules, 2018, individuals who hold or exercise significant beneficial ownership must declare it to the company (BEN-1), and the company files BEN-2 with the ROC. Where shares are held by a nominee, section 89 requires declarations and Form MGT-6. Founders holding through family members or holding companies should check these before diligence starts.

Common pitfalls

Cap tables kept only in spreadsheets

A spreadsheet that does not match the register of members and PAS-3 filings is the first finding in most reports. Reconcile to the statutory record.

Private placement shortcuts

Using application money before allotment, skipping the separate bank account, or missing PAS-4 are serious contraventions under section 42. Get advice on regularisation.

ESOP promises without a scheme

Option letters issued before the scheme is approved by special resolution are not valid grants. Approve the scheme first.

IP still with founders

If code or the brand was created before incorporation, the company may not own it until a written assignment is signed.

Late or missing FC-GPR

Foreign investment that was never reported is a FEMA issue. Regularise through late submission or compounding before the next round.

Frequently asked questions

What do investors check first in seed-stage due diligence?

Usually the cap table against the statutory record: register of members, share certificates, PAS-3 and SH-7 filings, and the resolutions approving each allotment. Then IP ownership, founder and employment agreements, the ESOP scheme and FEMA filings.

How do we fix a missed PAS-3 filing?

File it with the additional fee the MCA portal computes, and assess with an adviser whether any penalty or compounding is needed for the underlying default. If the allotment was a private placement, check every other section 42 step as well.

Do we need a valuation report for a seed round?

For a preferential allotment under section 62(1)(c), the rules require a valuation by a registered valuer. Where shares are issued to a non-resident, FEMA pricing rules apply and need certification as prescribed. Ask your adviser which reports your structure needs.

Can founders receive ESOPs in a startup?

Normally promoters and directors holding more than 10% cannot be granted ESOPs, but a relaxation applies to startups for a period from incorporation under rule 12 of the Share Capital and Debentures Rules. Confirm the current conditions before granting.
