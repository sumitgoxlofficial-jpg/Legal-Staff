When to choose which

Pick the option that fits your stage

Choose Paper diary or Excel

A paper diary can be enough when…

  • You appear in one or two courts with a modest number of matters
  • You are the only person who needs to see the diary
  • You prefer not to pay for software and are comfortable checking it every evening
Choose Legal Staff

Move to Legal Staff when…

  • You appear across several courts or tribunals and clashes are creeping in
  • Juniors and clerks need to see dates and tasks without calling you
  • You read many orders a week and want reminders that do not depend on memory
  • Clients regularly ask for the next date

The verdict

A paper diary is cheap, familiar and never runs out of battery. Its weakness is that everything depends on one person reading each order correctly, writing the date on the right page and remembering to look at it — and that only one copy exists.

Legal Staff keeps the habit of a daily list but takes the copying out of it. Orders are read and summarised, the next date is proposed for your confirmation, clashes are flagged and reminders go out without anyone having to send them. Many advocates keep a pocket diary for the day itself and let Legal Staff hold the record.

Whichever you use, keep checking the cause list of your court on the day: listings can change after an order is passed.

General information for advocates, not legal advice. Statutes, rules and court practice directions change; check the bare Act, the latest amendments and your court's rules before relying on any date or section.

Frequently asked questions

Can I keep my paper diary and use Legal Staff too?

Yes. Many advocates keep a pocket diary for the day and use Legal Staff as the record that the team, reminders and clients work from.

How long does it take to move my diary over?

You add your running matters and their next dates once. After that, each new order keeps the diary current once you confirm the date it picks out.

Does Legal Staff take dates from cause lists automatically?

No. Dates come from orders you paste or upload, or entries you make, and nothing reaches the diary until an advocate confirms it.

Is an Excel sheet not good enough?

A spreadsheet is searchable and can be shared, but it still needs every date typed in by hand and it sends no reminders. It also does not read orders, flag clashes or hold the case documents.

Does Legal Staff work on my phone?

Yes, in a mobile browser. It is a web app; there are no native iOS or Android apps.

What if I lose internet in court?

Legal Staff needs a connection. The morning briefing and reminders arrive before the day starts, so you will usually have the day’s list with you already.
