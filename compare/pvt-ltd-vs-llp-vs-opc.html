Private Limited vs LLP vs OPC: Which Structure Fits Your Business?

All three give you limited liability and a separate legal identity. They differ sharply in how profits are taxed, what you file every year and whether investors can come in. Here is the side-by-side view.

When to choose which

Pick the option that fits your stage

Choose Private Limited

You plan to raise money or hire with equity

  • You expect angel, VC or strategic investment
  • You want to grant ESOPs to employees
  • You may take foreign investors or set up a foreign-owned subsidiary
  • Customers or tenders expect a company
  • You want 115BAA's 22% base rate on retained profits
Choose LLP

Partners will run it and take the profits out

  • Professional practice, consulting or family business
  • Profits will be distributed rather than reinvested
  • You want the lightest annual compliance
  • Turnover may stay below the audit threshold early on
  • No plans for equity funding in the near term
Choose One Person Company

A solo founder who wants a company from day one

  • You are the only owner and an Indian citizen
  • You want limited liability and corporate status without a co-founder
  • You can name a nominee
  • You accept converting to a Private Limited when investors or co-founders join

Our view: start with the end state in mind

If outside equity is on your roadmap within two or three years, start as a Private Limited company. Converting an LLP later is possible, but it adds cost, paperwork and tax analysis exactly when you want to close a round. The compliance load of a small private company is manageable and the 115BAA regime makes the tax on retained profits competitive.

If the business will be owned and run by its partners and most profit will be distributed, an LLP usually costs less to maintain and avoids the second layer of tax on dividends. An OPC suits a solo Indian founder who wants a company structure now, with a clear plan to convert when co-founders or investors arrive.

Tax outcomes depend on your numbers, especially how much you will pay out as salary, remuneration or dividends. Run the figures for your expected profits before deciding, and confirm current rates for the tax year — the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026 and carries forward these regimes under new section numbers.

Frequently asked questions

Is an LLP cheaper to run than a Private Limited company?

Usually, yes. An LLP files two annual MCA forms, has no mandatory board meetings or AGM, and needs an audit only above ₹40 lakh turnover or ₹25 lakh contribution. A private company needs an audit every year and more filings, so its annual professional fees are typically higher.

Which pays less tax: a company or an LLP?

It depends on how profits are used. A company opting for section 115BAA pays about 25.17% on profits, but dividends are then taxed again in shareholders' hands. An LLP pays 30% plus surcharge and cess, but partners' profit share is exempt and remuneration within limits is deductible. If profits are retained for growth, a company often comes out ahead; if they are distributed, an LLP often does.

Can an OPC have more than one director?

Yes. An OPC has only one member, but it can have up to 15 directors (more with a special resolution). The single member is usually also a director.

Can a foreigner or NRI form an OPC?

Only a natural person who is an Indian citizen can form an OPC; since 2021 this includes Indian citizens who are not resident in India. Foreign nationals cannot be the member of an OPC and should consider a Private Limited company.

Can I convert my LLP into a Private Limited company later?

Yes, under section 366 of the Companies Act through Form URC-1, with the consent of partners and a no-objection from creditors. Tax neutrality on conversion depends on conditions in the Income-tax Act, so plan the conversion with your adviser before a funding round rather than during it.

Do startups need a Private Limited company to get DPIIT recognition?

No. Private Limited companies, LLPs and registered partnership firms can all be recognised. If you run an OPC, check eligibility carefully before applying; many OPC founders convert to a Private Limited company first, which also prepares them for investors.
