In-house Company Secretary vs Virtual Compliance Officer

A whole-time Company Secretary is a legal requirement for some companies and a luxury for most early-stage ones. Here is how the two models compare, and where the law draws the line.

When to choose which

Pick the option that fits your stage

Choose In-house CS / counsel

Hire full-time when the law or your scale demands it

  • Paid-up share capital is ₹10 crore or more, or you are listing
  • Board and investor activity is heavy and constant
  • You need someone on site for daily governance and contracts
  • You have the budget for salary, benefits and backup
Choose Virtual compliance officer

Go fractional while you are below the threshold

  • Paid-up share capital is below ₹10 crore
  • Compliance work is steady but not full-time
  • You want team cover instead of a single person
  • You want to add or drop scope as you grow
  • You need help fast, without a three-month hiring cycle

Our view: match the model to the mandate and the workload

Most startups and SMEs below ₹10 crore paid-up capital are not required to employ a whole-time Company Secretary, and their compliance workload rarely fills a full-time role. A virtual compliance officer delivers the calendar, filings and records at a fraction of the cost, with team cover.

Once the company crosses the paid-up capital threshold, lists, or reaches a level of board and investor activity that needs daily attention, a whole-time CS becomes either mandatory or sensible. At that stage a fractional team can still support the in-house CS with certifications, peak-season filings and specialist work.

Check the threshold against your latest paid-up capital after each allotment: an appointment is required once you cross it, and a vacancy in the office of whole-time KMP must be filled within six months.

General information, not legal or tax advice. Government fees, stamp duty and due dates are revised from time to time; confirm the current figures on the MCA, state stamp and Income Tax portals or with a practising professional before filing.

Frequently asked questions

When must a private company appoint a whole-time Company Secretary?

Under section 203 read with rule 8A of the Companies (Appointment and Remuneration of Managerial Personnel) Rules, 2014, a company with paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more must have a whole-time Company Secretary. Listed companies and public companies with paid-up capital of ₹10 crore or more must also appoint other whole-time KMP. Confirm the current rule before relying on it, as thresholds are revised from time to time.

What is the penalty for not appointing a required Company Secretary?

Section 203 provides penalties on the company and on every director and KMP in default, including a daily penalty for continuing default, subject to a maximum. It is cheaper to appoint on time than to regularise later.

Can a virtual compliance officer sign MCA forms?

Forms are signed by a director or authorised officer with their DSC. Where a form needs certification by a professional in practice, a practising CS, CA or CMA on the virtual team can certify it after verifying the records.

Is a virtual compliance officer suitable for a funded startup?

Often, yes, as long as paid-up capital is below the whole-time CS threshold. Funded startups have more allotments, ESOP actions and board meetings, so choose a retainer that includes them and agree response times for investor deadlines.

Does an in-house CS replace the need for a practising CS?

Not entirely. Certain certifications, such as MGT-8 for larger companies and secretarial audit where applicable, must be given by a Company Secretary in practice, not an employee.
