General information for advocates, not legal advice. Statutes, rules and court practice directions change; check the bare Act, the latest amendments and your court's rules before relying on any date or section. This free tool is a calculation aid: confirm every date and section against the bare Act before you file or cite.

Method

How this is calculated

  1. Source of the mapping. Each row pairs a section of the old law with the provision that replaced it in the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita or Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, checked against the text of the new Acts. Official comparative tables are also published by the Government (for example through the Bureau of Police Research & Development).
  2. Sub-sections matter. Many old sections are now sub-sections of a single new section — IPC 323 is BNS 115(2), IPC 379 is BNS 303(2). We show the sub-section that carries the punishment, because that is what goes into a charge, FIR or prayer clause.
  3. One old section, several new ones (and the reverse). Where an old provision was split or merged (IPC 506 into BNS 351(2) and 351(3); IEA 24–27 into BSA 22–23), the row says so in the notes. Read the full new section before relying on the number.
  4. No direct equivalent. Some provisions were dropped or replaced by differently worded offences — sedition (IPC 124A) is the main example: BNS 152 penalises acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India, which is not a renumbered 124A. Some BNS offences, such as organised crime (BNS 111), have no IPC equivalent.
  5. Rows marked "Check the official comparative table". Where the correspondence is partial or we are not fully certain of it, the row carries that badge. Verify those against the bare Act and the official table before citing them.
  6. Which law applies. The new Acts apply from 1 July 2024. Offences committed before that date are generally tried under the IPC (savings in BNS 358), and investigations, inquiries, trials and appeals pending on that date continue under the CrPC (BNSS 531). Check the savings clause for your stage of the case.

Frequently asked questions

What is IPC 420 in BNS?

Cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property (IPC 420) is now section 318(4) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, punishable with imprisonment up to seven years and fine. The definition of cheating is in BNS 318(1).

What is IPC 302 in BNS?

Punishment for murder under IPC 302 is now BNS 103(1): death or imprisonment for life, and fine. BNS 103(2) separately deals with murder by a group of five or more persons on grounds such as race, caste, community, sex, place of birth, language or personal belief.

Which BNSS section replaces CrPC 438 and 482?

Anticipatory bail (CrPC 438) is now BNSS 482, and the High Court's inherent powers (CrPC 482) are now BNSS 528. Because the number 482 now means anticipatory bail, write the Act name with the section in every petition.

Is there a BNS section for sedition (IPC 124A)?

No direct equivalent. The offence of sedition is not retained in the BNS. BNS 152 deals with acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India, but its ingredients are different, so do not treat it as a renumbered section 124A.

What replaced the section 65B certificate for electronic evidence?

Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 replaces IEA 65B. The certificate is in the form set out in the Schedule to the BSA, with Part A filled by the party producing the record and Part B by an expert.

Should I use IPC or BNS sections for an offence committed before 1 July 2024?

Generally the IPC, because the BNS applies to offences committed on or after 1 July 2024 and the repeal clause saves earlier proceedings. Pending CrPC investigations and trials also continue under the CrPC. Check the savings clauses (BNS 358, BNSS 531) and the latest rulings for your specific stage.
